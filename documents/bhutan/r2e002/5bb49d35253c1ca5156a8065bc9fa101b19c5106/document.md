## SSA Ratings Key

- 1 = The indicator is seldom or never found in the individual, classroom or the school. It is not a day to day norm, nor is it an impression that people would take away from a visit to this school (25%).

- 2 = The indicator is found in some individuals, classes, and sometimes  in  the  school.  It  is  not  regular  or  frequent -  most  classes  and  individuals  or  the  school  do  not demonstrate this. It is the exception, not the norm (50%).

- 3 = The indicator is found in most individuals, classes and at most of the times throughout the school. This is the norm in the school - not the exception (75%).

- 4  =  The  indicator  is  found  in  all  individuals,  classes  and throughout  the  school  at  all  times.  Any  visitor  to  the school will recognize this quality (100%).

## 3.	 School Performance Scorecards

- a Academic  learning  Scorecard  (ALS):  ALS  comprises of  results  of  classes  III,  VI,  X  and  XII  (percentage  of student performing above 45%,	60% and 70%) and the pass percentage of classes IX and XI.

- b Enabling  Practices  Scorecard  (EPS):  EPS  scorecard measures effectiveness of critical processes and practices of school management, quality and impact of teaching and training, infrastructure, and rigour and effectiveness of school planning processes.

- c Gross National Happiness Scorecard  (GNH):  GNH scorecard measures achievement on GNH values and practices  in  schools  as  measured  through  the  GNH indicators.

## Who will use school performance score cards?

Dzongkhag Education Officers, Assistant Dzongkhag Education Officers, Thromdey Education Officers and Assistant Thromdey Education Officers.

## How to use school performance score cards.

- DEOs/TEOs/ADEOs/ATEOs should visit the schools twice a year, once in the beginning of the academic year to assess on School Enabling Practices (EPS) and Gross National Happiness (GNH) scorecards. During the visit, DEOs/TEOs/ADEOs/ATEOs will confirm SSA and SIP ratings. The second visit should be made after the mid-term to review and assess SSA and SIP progress.

- In  the  process  of  assessment  and  review,  DEOs/TEOs/ ADEOs/ATEOs will identify areas for support and improvement.  They  have  to  prioritize  the  areas  of support  and  make  necessary  arrangements  to  avail support from relevant agencies.

- DEOs/TEOs/ADEOs/ATEOs should submit PMS current ratings of schools to EMSSD in the months of April-May and PMS review ratings in the months of September-October.

## Follow up actions on School PMS

- Annual data on PMS from Dzongkhags and Thromdeys are  collated  and  processed  by  the  EMSSD.  Data  from schools' EPS, GNH and ALS of classes III, VI, X and XII and the pass percentage of classes IX and XI are taken into account to generate a more comprehensive performance school ranking.

- Annual ranking of top ten schools are declared at four levels  of  schools  (III,  VI,  X  and  XII)  and  are  recognised through award of certificates.

- PMS  ranking  focuses  on  identifying  schools  that  need improvement and support.

- Reports of the consultative meetings with schools/ dzongkhags/ thromdeys and suitable recommendations are communicated to relevant organisational units in the Ministry of Education (MoE) for intervention and support.

## What  is  School  Performance  Management  System (PMS)?

PMS is a  system  of  continuous  improvement  at  all  schools which provides schools and Dzongkhags with tools to identify the needs for improvement and support such as professional development programmes, basic infrastructures and facilities, teacher  deployment,  maintaining  standard  student-teacher ratio, etc.

The  principle  of  PMS  is  to  create  transparency  in  school performance  with  the  focus  on  quality  of  education  and increase access to education to all.

## What are the key elements and tools of PMS?

PMS consists of three elements which are also used as tools for holistic assessment of schools. These are:

- School Self Assessment (SSA)

- School Improvement Plan (SIP) and

- School Performance Scorecards

- Academic Learning Scorecard (ALS)

- Enabling Practices Scorecard (EPS) and

- Gross National Happiness Scorecard (GNH)

## 1. What is School Self Assessment (SSA)?

SSA is a tool which is used by schools to reflect and assess their performance in a systematic and comprehensive manner. It takes into account the functioning of school leadership and management,  students  learning  outcome,  students  and teachers engagement and their wellbeing.

## 2. What is School Improvement Plan (SIP)?

SIP is a tool used by schools to prepare an improvement plan by setting targets for the academic year. SIP is prepared after the exercise on SSA where the schools are in a position to identify areas for improvement.

## Aims of SSA and SIP

- Is  to  assess  and  identify  strengths  and  weaknesses  of school;

- Is  to  review  and  develop  action  plans  to  improve  the school performance; and

- Is  to  guide  the  implementation  of  all  the  provisions on  a  continuous  basis  creating  enabling  conditions  to enhance learning.

## Key Areas for School Self Assessment

Teaching-learning of curriculum forms the main basis for  assessment.  However,  it  is  important  to  assess  other dimensions  which  contribute  to  intellectual  and  physical development of students. Therefore, SSA has the following six key areas with indicators for assessment:

- Leadership and Management practices;

- Green School Domain:

(i) Physical Ambience;

(ii) Psycho-Social Ambience;

- Curriculum Practices: Planning and Delivery;

- Holistic Assessment;

- Broader Learning Domain and

- School-Community Vitality.

## Who will use SSA?

Principal,  Teachers,  Students'  leaders,  School  Management Board and Office staff.

## How to use SSA:

- The  SSA  should  be  used  by  schools  TWICE  a  year.  It should be used in the beginning of the academic year to determine the current level of school performance and after mid-term to review the achievement of the first  term and reprioritize strategies and resources for the rest of the year.

- The  Principal,  teachers,  student  leaders,  office  staff, and members of the School Management Board (SMB) should rate the relevant indicators/key areas individually and later come to an agreement on the rating for each indicator.  The  agreed  ratings  should  be  recorded  in  a form  as  overall  school  rating  with  justifications  and evidences.

- The  indicators  of  Curriculum  Practices  and  Holistic Assessment  can  be  used  by  monitors  for  classroom observation to strengthen School Level Monitoring and Support Services (SLMSS).

- All the indicators in SSA should be achieved by creating an enabling environment.

## Who will use SIP?

Principal,  Teachers,  Students'  leaders,  School  Management Board and Office staff.

## How to use SIP:

- SIP should be developed in the beginning of the year by taking the current ratings of the identified indicators (measurable indicators) from the overall school ratings of SSA. It should be reviewed after the mid-term.

- The school should decide on the target ratings for the SIP indicators of the current year.

- All ratings should be in whole number.

- Develop specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time bound (SMART) actions to achieve the target of each indicator.

- Put a timeline for completion against each action,

- Reflect the person who is responsible for the actions to be implemented in the remarks column.

- Add any other indicators from SSA that you would like to improve upon. (Select not more than 2-5 indicators).

- The current ratings and SMART actions in SIP should be validated in the beginning of the year by DEOs/TEOs/ ADEOs/ATEOs.

- The  school  should  prepare  GNH  Progress  Wheel  by taking the average ratings of six key areas from SIP.

- The SIP should be reviewed and rated after the mid-term and review ratings are based on progress made by the school. These ratings are again validated by DEOs/TEOs/ADEOs/ATEOs during their second visit.

Note: The overall SSA ratings and SIP are used by DEOs/TEOs/ ADEOs/ATEOs during the PMS review exercise.

## GNH Progress Wheel

The SIP should be depicted as GNH Progress Wheel. While depicting GNH Progress Wheel, follow the steps given below:

- Transfer the average ratings of each of the six key areas from SIP into the GNH Progress Wheel;

- Plot the current ratings and join the points to show the current level of school performance web;

- Plot the target ratings to create the second web;

- Shade the area between two webs;

- During the review time, plot the third web to assess the progress. The lines can be in different colours to make it more attractive and distinct;
