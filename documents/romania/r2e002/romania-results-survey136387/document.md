-'Educated Romania' project (2021); As of September 2021, the country project 'Educated Romania' has become the reference document outlining the strategic framework for education policies up to 2030. The project 'Educated Romania' outlines a set of policies and a governance model for the national education system, clearly identifying the priority domains, the targets, objectives and lines of actions. The country project aims at achieving an equitable education system that ensures and respects the right of every pupil to quality education, having integrity, ethics and professionalism among its core pillars. In particular, one of the priority areas addressed by the Educated Romania project is that of quality inclusive education for all children, which aims to increase access to quality education for all social categories, especially for learners from disadvantaged backgrounds, to reduce the rate of early school leaving, to promote programmes to support the educational reintegration of dropouts, and to strengthen public policies on equity in education. Based on the policy framework set by the 'Educated Romania' project, a new legal framework for the field of education was developed in July 2023, consisting in Law No. 198/2023 on preuniversity education (hereinafter Law on preuniversity education) and Law No. 199/2023 on higher education (hereinafter Law on higher education). The two laws provide for principles that govern the education system, including the principles of nondiscrimination, quality, inclusion, the principle of ensuring equity and equal opportunities, the principles of guaranteeing the cultural identity of all Romanian citizens and intercultural dialogue, the principle of recognising and guaranteeing the rights of persons belonging to national minorities to preserve, develop and express their ethnic, cultural, linguistic and religious identity, the principle of focusing education on its primary beneficiaries, the principle of participation and responsibility of parents/legal representatives, the principle of flexibility/adaptability of the educational path, the principle of organising denominational education according to the specific requirements of each recognised religion, the principles of accessibility and availability in terms of guaranteeing access to quality education for all learners and respecting the right to education for all, the principle of the best interests of the pupil, etc. The provisions regarding the prohibition of discrimination and the equal access to education are presented in point 1.1 Nondiscrimination and equality in education. In line with the rights and principles of the Convention, the Law on pre-university education provides for the equal access, without any form of discrimination, to all levels and forms of pre-university education, to Romanian citizens, citizens of the Member States of the European Union, citizens of the Member States of the European Economic Area and of the Swiss Confederation, minors, who apply for or have acquired a form of protection in Romania, foreign minors and stateless minors whose residence on Romanian territory is officially recognised by law and citizens of non-EU countries legally residing on the Romanian territory, as well as British citizens and their family members as beneficiaries of the Agreement on the withdrawal of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland from the European Union and the European Atomic Energy Community 2019/C 384 I/01. The law provides for the right of primary beneficiaries of pre-university education (i.e. pre-school children (0-3 years), preschool children (3-6 years), pupils and adults enrolled in an educational establishment) of access to free quality education in the State education system. In addition, the law stipulates that state compulsory education, comprising preschool, primary, lower secondary and upper secondary education, is free of charge, although for some levels or forms of study fees may be charged under the conditions laid down by the law. The parents or legal guardians are obliged to ensure the attendance of the underage child for the entire period of compulsory education. Failure to comply with this obligation shall constitute an infringement and shall be penalized. The law provides for the organisation of secondchance programmes for primary, secondary and higher education for persons who have exceeded the age limit for the corresponding grade, regardless of whether or not they have been enrolled in the pre-university education system. These programmes are free of charge and of flexible duration. Pre-university education can be carried out in the languages of national minorities and persons belonging to national minorities have the right to study and be educated in their mother tongue at all levels, types and forms of pre-university education, under the conditions of the law. Educational establishments with instruction in the languages of national minorities shall be organised and operated in each administrative-territorial unit, where appropriate and in case this is not possible, the schooling of each pupil in his/her mother tongue shall be provided in the nearest administrative-territorial unit where such education can be provided. Any public or private legal entity interested in providing education may set up a pre-university education establishment. The evaluation procedures to be followed in order to obtain provisional authorisation to operate, accreditation or periodic external evaluation are based on national standards adopted by government decree and applicable to state, private and denominational pre-university education. The law allows the operation of private and denominational education organised on a non-profit basis in preuniversity education establishments. The criteria, quality standards and performance indicators to be met by private and denominational pre-university educational establishments are identical to those required of state educational establishments. The State supports and coordinates private and denominational education and provides the basic funding per pre-school/pupil for preuniversity education organised by accredited private and denominational educational establishments. Access to the teaching profession is not restricted. Teaching posts are filled without discrimination, on the basis of the results obtained in the competition for vacant teaching posts/chairs in state preuniversity education organized at national level. The Law on higher education provides that equal access to higher education is ensured, without any form of discrimination, to citizens of Romania, citizens of Member States of the European Union, citizens of States belonging to the European Economic Area and of the Swiss Confederation, as well as British citizens and their family members, as beneficiaries of the Agreement on the withdrawal of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland from the European Union and the European Atomic Energy Community 2019/C 384 I/01. Higher education is organized only in education providing organizations and higher education institutions that have been granted provisional operating authorization or accreditation in accordance with legal provisions. Higher education institutions may be state, private and denominational higher education institutions. The law provides that religious denominations officially recognized by the State have the right to organize denominational education by establishing and managing their own private educational institutions, in accordance with the provisions of the law. In addition, they may request the Ministry of Education to organise specific theological education within state education in existing state higher education institutions. In state higher education institutions, tuition is free of charge for the enrolment figure approved annually by Government Decision. For the places beyond the enrolment figure, tuition fees are charged. Tuition fees are charged in private and private denominational higher education institutions. In Romania, higher education is carried out including in the languages of national minorities (Art. 1(3)). The Law on higher education provides for the organization of higher education for national minorities. In line with policies promoted by the 'Educated Romania', both the Law on pre-university education and the Law on higher education strengthens the approach to inclusive education. Chapter V of the Law on Pre-University Education is dedicated to ensuring inclusive quality education for all primary beneficiaries of education, providing a set of measures, with funding from the state budget, such as the implementation by schools of programmes that support participation in preuniversity education, responding to the specific needs of pupils, with priority for pupils at risk of dropping out. Examples include the School after School program, which consists of educational activities to reinforce, remediate or deepen skills, as well as recreational activities, and the Remedial Learning program, designed to support students with learning difficulties or who are falling behind in learning. The law has also introduced the National Integrated Programme for Reducing School Drop-out, with subsidies for transport, social grants and school supplies as main pillars, together with programmes, activities and services to reduce absenteeism, drop-out and early school leaving at all levels of education. Gender equality is embedded in the Romanian education system. Girls and boys have equal access to the education system and enjoy equal rights. Discrimination based on gender is prohibited by law and it does not constitute an issue in the educational system. Law No. 202/2002 on equal opportunities for women and men prohibits any form of discrimination on the basis of sex in terms of access by women and men to all levels of education and vocational training, further training and, in general, continuing education. State and private educational institutions, social actors involved in instructionaleducational processes, as well as all other providers of training and further training services must include in their curricula, syllabuses and other curricular instruments measures to respect the principle of equal opportunities and treatment between women and men (Article 14). Article 15 stipulates that the Ministry of Education ensures, by specific means, training and education in the spirit of equal opportunities between the sexes, promoting school textbooks and university courses that do not include gender discrimination aspects, as well as negative behavioural patterns and stereotypes regarding the role of women and men in public and family life. As a specialized body of the central public administration, the National Agency for Equal Opportunities for Women and Men (NAEO), under the Ministry of Family, Youth and Equal Opportunities, implements the national strategies dedicated to the promotion of equal opportunities and equal treatment between women and men and to the prevention and combating of domestic violence. The strategies include measures for strengthening gender equality in the field of education. NAEO has organized information, awareness and sensitization campaigns among pupils, and students on the importance of respecting the right to non-discrimination and equal opportunities, but also on encouraging pupils to choose a career in STEM fields. The Law on pre-university education recognizes pregnant students /underage mothers as a category at risk of educational exclusion and consequently provides specific support measures aimed at ensuring the right to education and preventing school dropout. Article 79 of Law no 198/2023 on preuniversity education provides that the State guarantees equal access to education for pregnant pupils and for pupils who have become parents, without any kind of discrimination from members of the school community. Educational establishments have an obligation to take appropriate measures to protect pregnant and parenting pupils from stigmatization or discrimination, including by ensuring a safe and inclusive learning environment. Pregnant and parenting pupils benefit of a study program adapted to their needs, health care, including in school medical cabinets, remedial learning activities, including in the framework of the "School after School" and "Second Chance" programs; school, psychological and administrative counselling, the right to return to the educational establishment at any time during the school year and the right to access complementary services; financial support in case of financial difficulties (school supplies, clothes, other resources for participation in courses), priority for enrolment of the child in a nursery/ kindergarten close to the school, postponement of exams when they overlap with the child's period of childbirth/care. Pregnant/ parenting pupils have a monitor teacher who is responsible for supervising their educational path, staying in contact with the school and proposing educational measures in their best interest. In addition, underage mothers reintegrated in a preuniversity educational establishment receive a monthly allowance during the period of teaching activities, provided they attend classes. Regarding STEM education, Law on pre-university education foresees the establishment of a laboratory for computer science, science, technology, engineering, art and mathematics in every school. Support and affirmative measure to ensure the right to education of refugee children and youth from Ukraine

The Romanian legislative framework provides adequate support for the enrolment and schooling of migrants, refugees, asylumseekers, stateless persons and internally displaced persons in the Romanian education system and for learning the Romanian language. As mentioned in Section 1.1 Nondiscrimination and equality in education, minors who apply for or have acquired a form of protection in Romania, foreign minors and stateless minors whose residence on Romanian territory is officially recognised by law and citizens of non-EU countries legally residing on Romanian territory enjoy equal access, without any form of discrimination, to all levels and forms of pre-university education in Romania on equal footing with the Romanian citizens. In view of facilitating their integration and schooling, free introductory language courses are provided during the school year through the network of county school inspectorates, subordinated to the Ministry of Education. The introductory course is delivered following a specially designed curriculum approved by order of the Minister of Education. At the same time, the teachers have at their disposal textbooks approved by the Ministry of Education, which are provided free of charge to both teachers and pupils. Romanian language introductory courses are also provided to adult foreigners who obtain a form of international protection or a right of residence in Romania. The courses are also organized by the county school inspectorates following a specific curriculum and using the corresponding textbooks approved by the Ministry of Education, which are available free of charge for the beneficiaries of international protection. The enrolment in pre-university education is done based on a request submitted to the county school inspectorates and a subsequent procedure for the recognition and equivalence of studies. Until the completion of this procedure, the concerned minors, as well as young people over the age of 18, who have not finalized their pre-university education, have the right to attend classes in pre-university educational institutions as auditors/attendees, as well as to participate in school and extra-school activities. In order to overcome the difficulties of adaptation, both while attending the Romanian language initiation course or while attending school classes as attendees/listeners, as well as later, during schooling, minors and young people benefit from the assistance of school counsellors. It is worth mentioning that the Law on preuniversity education reinforces inclusive education and, in this context, it explicitly defines the categories of primary beneficiaries at risk of school exclusion, for whom the law provides additional support measures and which includes the refugees. In 2022 the regulatory framework concerning the integration measures for minor and adult foreigners who have acquired a form of international protection or a right of residence in Romania was updated. As such, the following orders of the minister of education were issued: -Order No. 5807 of 29 September 2022 on the approval of the special methodology for the attestation of pre-university studies for foreigners who have obtained international protection in Romania and who do not have documents proving their studies or who, for objective reasons, do not fall within the current legal provisions in the field of recognition and attestation of studies of foreigners in Romania, in order to facilitate access to the labour market or the continuation of studies -Order No 6127 of 24 October 2022 for the approval of the Methodology for the schooling and the organisation and conduct of the Romanian language initiation course and of the Procedure for the elaboration, approval and distribution of the Romanian language initiation course programmes and manuals for minors who have acquired a form of international protection or a right of residence in Romania, as well as for minors who are citizens of the Member States of the European Union and of the European Economic Area and of the Swiss Confederation -Order No 6129 of 24 October 2022 for the approval of the methodology for the organisation and conduct of the Romanian language initiation course and of the procedure for the elaboration, approval and distribution of the Romanian language initiation course programmes and manuals for adult foreigners who have acquired a form of international protection or a right of residence in Romania, as well as for citizens of the Member States of the European Union and of the European Economic Area and citizens of the Swiss Confederation By Order no. 6.127/2022 and Order no. 6.129/2022 respectively, the number of hours per week for the Romanian language introductory course increased from 4 to 6 hours/week. Each learner receives a minimum of 216 hours which correspond to period of 36 weeks spread over one school year and, where appropriate, the following school year. Learners may continue to attend the course after the completion of 216 hours, until the first examination session of the Romanian language skills acquired, which they are allowed to take. The Law on higher education stipulates that the state supports, including financially, the participation in adult education and vocational training of refugees, as well as the recognition and certification of the learning outcomes and competences acquired by them (Art. 184). In order to facilitate the integration of refugees, as of May 2022, the National Centre for the Recognition and Equivalence of Diplomas in Romania (CNRED) has introduced the European Qualifications Passport for Refugees (EQPR) into the national legislation. Thus, persons enjoying international protection on Romanian territory who cannot provide proof of studies or qualifications can apply for the EQPR, which is issued in English under the aegis of the Council of Europe. The document is not an official act of recognition, but a recommendation, which is valid 5 years. In addition, on the basis of the EQPR, CNRED can issue a recommendation for access to higher education and labour market in Romanian. Education for people with disabilities and special education needs (SEN) is provided through special education and integrated special education, which is an integral part of the national education system in Romania, being free of charge and usually organized as full-time education. Children with SEN are those who, due to mental, psychological, intellectual and/or sensory disabilities, as well as physical, motor and neuro-motor disabilities, emotional, neurodevelopmental, social maladjustment or any other disease, disorder or chronic, genetic condition, need resources and specialized support. SEN also includes children with specific learning disabilities: dyslexia, dysgraphia, dysorthographia, and dyscalculia. Special and integrated special education is a form of differentiated, adapted schooling and a form of comprehensive educational, social and medical support for people with SEN. Integrated special education is organised within mainstream education, by including every pre-schooler and pupil with SEN in mainstream groups, classes or study groups. Special education and special integrated education therefore are addressed to: · primary beneficiaries with SEN integrated into mainstream education who follow the mainstream education curriculum; · primary beneficiaries with special needs integrated in special classes/groups organised in mainstream education who follow the mainstream or special education curriculum; · primary beneficiaries with special education needs who follow the special education curriculum; · p rimary beneficiaries with special needs requiring periods of hospitalisation of more than 4 weeks for whom "School in Hospital" is organised; · primary beneficiaries with SEN who, for medical reasons or because of a disability, are not mobile, for whom home schooling is organised for a fixed period. The Law on PreUniversity Education has strengthened the provision of special education. Special education is regulated by articles 68 -71 of Chapter V on Quality inclusive education to all primary beneficiaries of education. The law provides that in ante pre-primary education (for children aged 0 to 3 years old), special education is organised in the form of early intervention groups. The mode of organization, the therapies and the early intervention services are provided by a methodology approved by the joint order of the minister of education, the minister of health and the minister of family, youth and equal chances. In compulsory pre-university education, special education is to be provided on the basis of a 4-level support system, from pre-school education, as it follows: - Special Level I - Basic; - Level II special support Supplementary; - Level III special support Intensive; - Level IV special support Special. Special education is provided from pre-school up to and including post-school level, differentiated according to the specific special educational needs, by adapting the school curriculum and supplementing the teaching-learning-assessment process with specific interventions, as well as through complementary activities. For the primary beneficiaries identified with special educational needs a "Personalized Educational Plan" is elaborated by the county psycho-pedagogical assistance centre's which also includes psychological services and psycho-pedagogical assistance services, learning support services, specific therapies, physiotherapy, as well as family services. The provision of educational and assistance services to primary beneficiaries with SEN, as well as for the implementation of the individualized service plan, are provided a framework methodology initiated by the Ministry of Education and approved by Government Decision. Special education and integrated special education have curricula, school programmes, psycho-pedagogical assistance programmes, textbooks and alternative teaching methodologies, as well as assessment methods adapted to the type and severity of the disability and which are approved by order of the Minister of Education. Specific psycho-pedagogical methods are employed in the education of primary beneficiaries with specific learning disorders, such as dyslexia, dysgraphia, dysorthography, and dyscalculia, in line with a methodology approved by order of the Minister of Education. Mainstream educational establishments may collaborate with accredited faculties of psychology and pedagogical high schools, so that students and trainees of those faculties or high schools can assist students with SEN and/or disabilities during class time. The duration of schooling for pupils with SEN may be extended by up to 3 years, in which case the duration is determined based on the individual needs, type and severity of the disability and SEN. For pupils with special educational needs, equal opportunities are provided, according to the type of disorder/disability, both during the learning process and when taking the national assessment at the end of grade 8, the national baccalaureate exam and the professional skills/qualification certification exams in accordance with the methodology approved by order of the Minister of Education. Primary beneficiaries of level I, II, III and IV may acquire professional qualifications appropriate to the type and degree of disability in special and mainstream education establishments, in consultation with local stakeholders. Special secondary schools, including special technological schools, can offer pupils a level 3 or 4 qualification according to the National Qualifications Framework and direct access to the labour market on the basis of specific vocational tests or a vocational qualification certification examination. The Ministry of Education, in collaboration with the Ministry of Labour and Social Solidarity and other responsible authorities, establishes the legal framework for encouraging and facilitating access and participation on the labour market and/or higher education for primary beneficiaries with SEN. Adult education is any form of non-vocational education after the initial education stage, whether formal, non-formal or informal. As provided by the Law on pre-university education, in the initial teacher training a great emphasis is now put on practical training/ classroom practice, flexibility and supporting teachers at professional debut. Teaching staff in pre-university education is obliged to participate at least once every 2 years in at least one accredited in-service training program, according to a plan established at the level of the educational establishment, based on an analysis of training needs. Expenses for in-service training of teaching staff in pre-university education are borne by the employer, from sums allocated from the state budget or from other sources of funding according to the law, at least for obtaining the minimum number of ECTS credits required by law. To improve teachers' competences and consequently the quality of teaching, in-service teacher training programmes have been carried out as part of large-scale projects, while other are in implementation or in preparatory phase. In the national curriculum human rights education is approached both as a standalone discipline (compulsory/core discipline and elective/ optional discipline, as the case may be) and in an integrated manner, by including relevant topics in other disciplines, to help develop appropriate attitudes and behaviours in children and pupils. This latter integrated approach is used starting from pre-school education. The early childhood education curriculum, adopted in 2019, is based on principles such as the respec t for the child's right, the principle of equity and nondiscrimination and that of interculturality. The curriculum promotes and fosters the development of positive social behaviours, the respect and acceptance of diversity, within the social-emotional development domain. In designing the educational activities teachers help children lean and respect the rules and understand their role in social and family relations, take up responsibilities according to their age, exercise their ability to negotiate and take decisions, show acceptance and understanding towards other people. At primary level, pupils study Civic Education taught in the 3rd and 4th grade as a core discipline. The syllabus for 4th grade includes teaching about the universal rights of the children and activities that promote these rights. In lower secondary school, pupils study the core discipline Social Education, which treats a specific topic in each grade. In respect to human rights education, pupils' study Critical thinking and the Rights of the Child in the 5th grade, Intercultural Education in the 6th grade and Education for Democratic Citizenship in the 7th grade. It should be noted that the design of the primary and lower secondary education curriculum has used as reference point for all relevant disciplines the Council of Europe's Reference Framework of Competences for Democratic Culture. With regard to the optional disciplines, schools can provide pupils additional disciplines that approach human rights education as a standalone subject or in an integrated manner. The sch ools' offer of optional disciplines can include disciplines designed at national, local, or school level. The relevant optional disciplines designed at national level include Education for society (primary education), Character first! (Primary education and gymnasium), Ready for life. Education for life and community (gymnasium); Civic culture (gymnasium), Skills for adolescence (gymnasium), Human rights (high school), Intercultural education (high school). The Ministry of Education supports, including financially, the organisation and carrying out of educational competitions and contests at local, county and national level, dedicated to education for active citizenship education, such as: -Civic Education Olympiad for grades 3 and 4 and the Civic Culture Olympiad for grades 7 and 8, in which teams of two pupils can participate. The Romanian education system offers multiple educational activities carried out outside the formal-curricular education system on an annual basis (projects, contests, festivals, and other programmes).
