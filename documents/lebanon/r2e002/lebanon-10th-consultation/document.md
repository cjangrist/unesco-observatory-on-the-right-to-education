The Ministry of Education and Higher Education is among the ministries active and effective in strengthening the right to education, especially as regards combating discrimination in education, and has done the following:

# I. Commitment under the Lebanese Constitution and legislation, which enshrine the right to education

## The Lebanese Constitution :
Article 10 of the Lebanese Constitution, promulgated on 23 May 1926 provides: 'Education is free, provided it does not disturb public order or violate morals  and  does  not  compromise  the  dignity  of  any  religion  or  creed.  The  rights  of communities to establish their own private schools may not be violated, provided that they comply with the general requirements laid down by the State with respect to public education'.

## 2. Lebanon has adopted a number of Acts organizing citizens' right to education

(a) Legislative Decree No. 134 of 12/6/1959, which introduced 'free education'.

(b) Act No. 686 of 16/3/1998 established the principle of compulsory, free primary education, although the implementing decree of that Act has not yet been issued.

(c) In addition, the right to education of all persons with disabilities was established under article 24 of the Persons with Disabilities Act No. 220 of 2000.

(d) The Advancement of Education in Lebanon Plan of 1994.

(e) The new structure of education authorized under Cabinet Decision No. 22 of 25/10/1995.

(f) The new curriculum issued under Decree No. 10227 of 8/5/1997.

# III. Advancement of Education in Lebanon Plan

The Advancement of Education in Lebanon Plan, which was adopted by the Cabinet in Decision No. 15/94 of 17/8/1994, aimed to educate the younger generation in the necessary knowledge,  expertise  and  skills,  and  to  strengthen  national  education  and  deep-rooted Lebanese values such as freedom, democracy, tolerance and rejection of violence, and also to  strengthen  youth,  sporting  and  artistic  activities.  This  plan  also  contributed  to  the rehabilitation of the education system. It was an important step towards starting to reform it, including  through  the  new  education  curricula  issued  in  1997  and  their  adoption  after  the curricula had remained unchanged since 1968.

# IV. Gender perspective

- One of the tasks of the Gender Perspective Committee chaired by the Director-General of Education is to integrate the gender perspective in public policy for the Ministry of Education  and  Higher  Education,  and  to  organize  training  courses  and  discussion seminars in the Ministry for officers, students, administrative and teaching staff, and parents  committees  on  the  concept  of  the  gender  perspective,  what  it  is  and  the mechanism  for  integrating  it  in  the  Ministry  plan,  and  also  on  the  International Declaration of Human Rights, child rights law and the Convention on the Elimination of all Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), free, compulsory education and the mechanisms for implementing it, and other matters.

- In  addition  to  its  educational  efforts  focusing  on  research  and  development,  the Ministry of Education and Higher Education is preparing studies that adopt the gender perspective to analyse education curricula.

- Some teachers have been trained in cooperation with the directorates of the Ministry of Education and Higher Education, and in particular with guidance and orientation, through activities and the texts and pictures in school textbooks.

# V. Sustainable Development Goal 4 of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development

- The Ministry of Education and Higher Education is working to strengthen the Education 2030 Strategy.

- A committee chaired by the Minister of Education and Higher Education is promoting the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, and especially Sustainable Development Goal 4: 'Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong  learning  opportunities  for  all'.  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education Steering Committee.

- The Ministry of Education and Higher Education guarantees education for all students, including persons with disabilities, without any official evaluation measures.

They  enjoy  the  support  of  the  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher Education, just as government schools do, but are supervised by a joint committee, in tandem with the Ministry of Social Affairs, that brings together representatives of those educational establishments and determines the working conditions and the costs of the education in them.

# VI. Enrolment of students with a Lebanese mother married to a foreigner

We have already explained that Lebanon's duty to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals (2015-2030) required the Ministry of Education and Higher Education to strengthen the right to education for all. Furthermore, the vision of the National Education Strategy assigns to  the  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  the  task  of  enrolling  students  with  a Lebanese mother married to a foreigner, giving them the necessary attention, treating them like all students and not dismissing them during study and giving school places to all who are interested according to the rules. We recall, for example, Decision No. 415/m/2020 of 16/9/2020 approving the enrolment of non-Lebanese students in government schools and secondary  schools  for  the  academic  year  2020/2021  and  extending  the  deadline  for enrolment of Lebanese students in afternoon classes (appended to this report).

The Ministry of Education and Higher Education is working to strengthen the right to education for all through:

(a) The Ministry is promoting the Education 2030 Strategy.

(b) A committee chaired by the Minister of Education and Higher Education is promoting the 2030  Agenda  for  Sustainable  Development,  and  especially  Sustainable  Development Goal 4: 'Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all'. Ministry of Education and Higher Education Steering Committee.

(c)  The  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  ensures  education  for  all  students, including non-Lebanese who are the children or husbands of Lebanese women.

(d) The Ministry of Education and Higher Education does not demand any payment from nonLebanese  students  who  are  the  children  or  husbands  of  Lebanese  women  at  either primary or secondary school.

(e) The Ministry of Education and Higher Education works constantly to promote Goal 4 of the  2030  Agenda  for  Sustainable  Development.  In  this  connection,  we  point  to  the decision issued by the Minister of Education and Higher Education No. 401/m/2020 of 7/9/2020  approving  the  enrolment  of  students  with  a  Lebanese  mother  married  to  a foreigner (appended to this report) under which all those responsible for government and private primary and secondary schools and institutes are requested to give the necessary attention to enrolling students whose mother is a Lebanese woman married to a foreigner, to treat them as Lebanese students and provide them with school places.

With regard to afternoon school / the second session , the same education is provided as in morning school / the first session. The successful children enrolled between year one and year nine obtain the official certificate recognized by the Ministry of Education and Higher Education, while the pupils in the years nine and twelve may sit the official examinations.

With regard to the various educational programmes in Lebanon facilitating the entry of non-Lebanese students in official/public schools, the most important are:

(a) Accelerated  learning  programme. A  separate,  informal  programme  that  qualifies non-Lebanese pupils for enrolment in government schools. It was developed to provide teaching to children aged between seven and 17 years who have been out of school for more than two years to prepare the way for their integration into formal Lebanese education.

The  list  of  schools  offering  this  programme  is  available  from  UNICEF,  the  United Nations  High  Commission  for  Refugees,  social  development  centres  and  on  the Internet at WWW.refugees-lebanon.org and the website of the Ministry of Education and Higher Education WWW.MEHE.gov.lb

At the end of the programme, the children are able to be integrated in government education in Lebanon. At the start and at the end of the programme, the children are assessed  as  to  whether  they  are  ready  to  reintegrate  the  government  education system the following year.

(b) Other education programmes, known as 'informal education programmes' , are also provided on the basis of the needs of the children concerned. The programmes are  offered  by  non-governmental  organizations  (NGOs)  and  do  not  bestow  a recognized certificate. With the support of the United Nations, the Ministry of Education and Higher Education draws up a working framework for informal education enabling NGOs to implement informal education programmes it has approved and recognized.

(c) Support  with  homework  assignments is  provided  to  some  students  enrolled  in formal education in government schools who attend regularly but are at risk of dropping out.  This  support  is  provided  at  specified  times  and  in  different  frameworks.  The students study their textbooks and receive support that allows them to succeed in the school year. These programmes have sometimes been held in centres coming under the Ministry of Education and Higher Education for students enrolled in government schools who need this type of support.

If  Lebanese  or  non-Lebanese  children  have  educational  difficulties ,  they  can enrol in government schools, including if they have special needs. Contact is made through  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  hotlines  so  that  they  are transferred to specialized programmes.

It should be noted that UNRWA provides educational services (from year one to year 12) to Palestinian refugees living in Lebanon , including Palestinian refugees arriving from Syria. UNRWA provides free education, in addition to books and a 'return to  school'  mechanism.  It  presents  students  in  schools  supported  by  UNRWA  in Lebanon for the same government examinations as Lebanese pupils, so refugees and migrants in Lebanon who have completed the twelfth year in their schools or in the country of origin are able to enrol at government or affiliated private universities in Lebanon.

# VII. Effectiveness of the right to education of persons with disabilities and implementation of the National Plan for the Integration of Persons with Special Needs

The Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  recognizes  that  disability  is  still  a developing concept. It is the result of the interaction between persons with a disability and  barriers  in  situations  and  surrounding  environments  that  prevent  their  full  and active  participation  in  society  to  the  same  extent  as  others.  It  also  recognizes  the importance of the global character of all the human rights and fundamental freedoms provided  for  in  the  International  Declaration  of  Human  Rights  and  international conventions on human rights, and the need to ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy these rights fully and without discrimination, considering integration of the issues of persons with disabilities to be an inseparable part of the sustainable development strategy.

In this context, it should be noted that making effective the right to education of persons with disabilities requires the strengthening of the education system in its entirety at every level. The educational path of these persons needs a great deal of time if it is to result in effectively achieving the right to obtain an education for all Lebanese children without distinction.

To achieve the desired goal of this system, we wish to review examples of the measures  taken  by  the  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  in  this regard.

(a)  Since the adoption of Act No. 220/2000 on the rights of persons with disabilities, the Lebanese Government, through the Ministry of Social Affairs, has allocated credits  for  the  benefit  of  persons  with  special  needs  and  their  education  in specialized establishments once their conditions have been identified in centres for persons  with  disabilities  and  a  programme  ensuring  the  rights  of  persons  with disabilities that comes under the Ministry of Social Affairs, where there are doctors, and a group of specialists, including educational assessors, speech therapists and sensorimotor  therapists.  The  Ministry  of  Education  and  Higher  Education  is responsible for ensuring education to all students without an official evaluation, drawing  up  individual  plans  and  supervising  their  implementation,  preventing overlap with the Ministry of Social Affairs.

Taking as the starting-point the right of persons with special needs to education, this requires the education system in its entirety to be strengthened at every level and  it  will  take  a  long  time  for  them  to  be  able  to  obtain  education  without discrimination. The General Directorate of Compulsory Education of the Ministry of Education and Higher Education is taking steps to respond to the requirements at different levels, above all the work of the committee to determine the conditions of those  with  special  educational  needs  concerning  official  examinations  for  the intermediate  certificate,  on  the  one  hand,  and,  on  the  other,  studying  cases  of special educational needs and pathological conditions. This committee recommends what is commensurate with each pathological condition before it in relation to the examination for the intermediate certificate, after studying the case file  and  meeting  each  student.  It  then  issues  a  decision  of  exemption  from  the intermediate certificate in the cases that so require before the beginning of the ordinary course for the official examinations and continues its work until the end of the  exceptional  course  of  each  year  of  study.  In  any  case,  as  the  official examinations  are the fundamental  expression  of the national  assessment operation  of  students,  including  those  with  educational  difficulties  and  special needs, rooms in centres are allocated for examinations so that they are nearby and facilitate student access to examination centres.

Note: The Ministry of Education and Higher Education does not impose any additional expense or fees on students with special educational needs or difficulties, however severe.

(b)  Regarding  the  issue  of  the  enrolment  of  students  with  special  needs  and educational difficulties, the Ministry of Education and Higher Education has not yet issued  any  memorandum,  decision  or  official  document  refusing  to  accept  any student  wishing  to  enrol  in  a  school.  The  General  Education  Directorate  has prepared a draft implementing decree for the Compulsory Education Act No. 150 of 17/8/2011, stipulating that disability does not in and of itself constitute a sufficient impediment to exclude a pupil from school, and that all the circumstances set out in article 60 of Act No. 220/2000 shall be taken into account before deciding to review  how  a  child  with  a  disability  is  to  be  educated.
