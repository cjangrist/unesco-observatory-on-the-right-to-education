## 1. Introduction

The Convention against Discrimination in Education ('the Convention') and the Recommendation against Discrimination in Education ('the Recommendation'), adopted by UNESCO's General Conference in 1960, are  aligned  with  UNESCO's  constitutional  mandate  to  'advance  the  ideal  of  equality  of  educational opportunities without regard to race, sex or any distinctions, economic or social'. The Convention is the first legally  binding  international  instrument  focused  on  the right  to  education and  the  only  one  exclusively dedicated to it.

The right to education is one of the key principles underpinning the Education 2030 agenda and Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG4) adopted by the international community in 2015. SDG 4 seeks to ensure the full  enjoyment  of  the  right  to  education  as  fundamental  to  achieving  sustainable  development.  Its  scope captures the multidimensional nature of the right to education.

Representing a powerful tool to advance SDG 4 by ensuring effective articulation between normative and operational work, the Convention has been recognized as a cornerstone of Education 2030 agenda.

The present  Guidelines  are  intended  to  assist  Member  States  in  the  preparation  of  country  reports  on  the implementation of the Convention and the Recommendation in the context of the 11th consultation.

## 2. Scope of the Consultation

Considering the Education 2030 agenda, the monitoring of the Convention and the Recommendation is of crucial importance to ensure an education that is truly inclusive for all and to counter persistent inequalities in access, participation and learning outcomes at all levels, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. This is  particularly  true  under  the  current  context  in  which  countries  are  making  efforts  to  recover  from  the learning disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. Concrete measures taken by countries in accordance with their legal obligations under these instruments contribute to implementing the Education 2030 agenda.

As the scope and content of the legal obligations under the right to education are aligned with the 10 targets of SDG 4, efforts are being made to ensure that the components of the targets are included in the guidelines and that these guidelines serve as a valuable contribution to monitoring progress towards SDG 4 targets.

Beyond SDG 4, the Convention is also closely aligned with the normative content on the right to education of other international human rights instruments such as the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural  Rights  (1966),  Convention  on  the  Elimination  of  All  Forms  of  Discrimination  against  Women (1979), the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) and the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006).

In addition, this Consultation is an opportunity to inform the ongoing UNESCO Initiative on the Evolving Right to Education within a lifelong learning perspective 1 (launched in December 2021). The Initiative investigates how the right to education could be reinforced placing it within a lifelong learning perspective and in light of the new demands being placed on education. This Initiative builds on the Report from the International Commission on the Futures of Education 2 , which suggests to broaden our understanding of the right to education to clearly apply it to all ages and across all times and spaces. The initiative is also inline with the Vision Statement of the United Nations Secretary General on Transforming Education 3 , and  the Report  on  the  2022  Transforming  Education  Summit 4 which  call  for  the  transformation  of education systems to 'include everyone, regardless of circumstance, context or background'. The 11th consultation builds on the findings of the previous national reports to include various areas that have direct repercussions on the right to education such as, for instance, digital learning, climate considerations, lifelong learning entitlement, and the role of non-state actors in education.

## 3. Reporting obligations

There are differences in the legal nature of the obligations under the Convention, as distinguished from those under the Recommendation. The Convention has binding force and the States Parties are under the obligation to incorporate its provisions into their domestic frameworks. The Recommendation, non-binding in nature, has political and moral force. Its adoption sought to take into account the difficulties that certain States might experience in ratifying the Convention, in particular with respect to their federal structure.

Bearing in mind the differences in legal scope inherent to the nature of these two categories of instruments, the content of the Recommendation is identical to that of the Convention. Member States are expected to give effect to it in national legislation and education policies.

The reporting obligation under the provisions of Article VII of the Convention and of the Recommendation is  the  same.  Article  VIII  of  UNESCO's  Constitution  states  that  ' Each Member State shall  submit to the Organization, at such times and in such manner as shall be determined by the General Conference, reports on  the  laws,  regulations  and  statistics  relating  to  its  educational,  scientific  and  cultural  institutions  and activities,  and  on  the  action  taken  upon  the  recommendations  and  conventions  referred  to  in  Article  IV, paragraph 4' . Therefore, even if a country is not a State Party to the Convention, it needs to submit a report on  the  implementation  of  the  Recommendation,  which  means  that all  UNESCO  Member  States  are expected  to  submit  a  national  report on  the  implementation  of  these  instruments  in  the  context  of monitoring consultations.

The Futures of Education Report calls for a new social contract for education, which requires to understand education  as  a  common  good  and  a  shared  societal  endeavour.  To  ensure  this,  extensive  consultations involving a broad range of stakeholders is needed. The  reporting process therefore needs to be participatory. The  preparation  of  national  reports  provides  a good  opportunity for  Member  States  to  assess  the implementation  of  the  right  to  education  in  their  country,  identify  specific  challenges,  build  constructive dialogue with other actors involved in this field and share good practices.

In  the  preparation  of  reports,  Member  States  are  encouraged  to  provide substantive  and  relevant information  in  a  concise  manner . In addition to a description of the constitutional, legislative, administrative, and policy framework established for the implementation of the provisions guaranteed by the instruments, the information supplied by Member States should demonstrate how legal norms and practices comply with the Convention and the Recommendation.

Relevant information supplied for other reporting instances may be reproduced or referred to with precise indication as to its retrieval. Similarly, hyperlinks to any useful documents or relevant legal and policy texts are most welcome and texts can also be added in the annex.

Country  reports  submitted  for  previous  consultations  can  be  found  on  the Observatory  on  the  right  to education 7 in  the  monitoring  section  of  the  country  profile. Information and data regarding countries ' education systems are also available through the UNESCO Institute for Statistics database 9 .  Beyond monitoring purposes, the consultation findings and reports inform broader work  - serving for knowledge generation, advocacy, and technical cooperation.

## 5. Modalities and structure

-The first chapter covers general information on adherence to the Convention.

-The second  chapter aims  to  gather  information  on  the  implementation  of  the  Convention  and Recommendation in the context of Sustainable Development Goal 4. As the most extensive part of the Guidelines, this chapter covers: inclusion and non-discrimination in education (section 1), all levels and forms of education (section 2), quality education provision and learning environment (section 3) and finally, strengthening governance systems (section 4).

-The third  chapter seeks  to  obtain  information  on  the  methods  used  to  draw  the  attention  of  the various authorities in the country to the instruments.

##### Anti-discriminatory and affirmative legislation in the field of education

The Ministry of Education pay special attention to the prevention of discrimination in the system of education and upbringing. Violence, abuse and neglect are prohibited under the Law on Fundamentals of System of Education and Upbringing -the LFSEU (Official Gazette of the RS, No. 88/17, 27/18 (other law), 27/18 (other law), 10/19, 6/20, 129/21, 92/23) in educational institutions (Article 110); violence, abuse and neglect are prohibited (Article 111); behaviours whereby reputation, honour and dignity  of  the  children/pupils/students,  employees  and  parents  are  damaged  are  prohibited.  In  cases  of  breaches  of  Articles relating to prohibition, the institutions are obliged to act by following the prescribed procedure -https://www.paragraf.rs/propisi/zakon_o_osnovama_sistema_obrazovanja_i_vaspitanja.html.

The elements for identification  of  discriminatory  behaviour  and  the  procedures  for  actions  for  the  educational  institutions  in cases of suspected or determined discriminatory conduct among pupils/students, students and staff members, among the staff members, parents and other adults are regulated quite precisely by both the Rulebooks. In addition to that, both the Rulebooks have brought the definition of discrimination which is in conformity with the Convention against Discrimination in Education from 1960:

'Discrimination and/or discriminatory behaviour, within the meaning of the Law and this Act, shall mean the conduct having th e purpose of either directly or indirectly, openly or covertly, unjustifiably making distinction or impairing equality in treatment and/or omitting to act (exclusion, limitation or preference), in relation to a person or a group of persons, as well as members of their families or the persons close to them, which is based on race, colour of the skin, ancestors, citizenship, status of a migrant and/or displaced person, nationality or ethnic origin, language, religious or political convictions, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation,  financial  standing,  social  and  cultural  origin,  birth,  genetical  characteristics,  health  condition,  intellectual impairments and disability, marital and family status, conviction, age, appearance, membership in political, union and other organisations  and  other  actual  and/or  presumed  personal  characteristics,  as  well  as  on  other  bases  laid  down  by  the  law prescribing non-discrimination. '

In  addition  to  that,  according  to  the  LFSEU,  the  nationals  of  the  Republic  of  Serbia  shall  be  equal  in  exercising  the  right  to education; persons with disabilities shall have the right to education and upbringing whereby their educational needs and needs for upbringing shall be taken into account, by stressing the individual and/or group additional support in teaching and learning. The  foreign  nationals,  as  well  as  the  stateless  persons  and  the  persons  who  are  citizenship  seekers  shall  have  the  right  to education and upbringing under the same conditions and in the manner prescribed for the nationals of the Republic of Serbia. According to Article 4 of that Law, 1) upbringing and education of children in the year immediately preceding the beginning of their schooling; 2) the primary education and upbringing of pupils and adults; as well as 3) the secondary education of full-time and part-time students shall be provided free of any charge, under equal conditions. The Law further on provides for equality and availability of exercising of the right to education and upbringing based on social justice and the principle of equal opportunities without  discrimination,  respect  for  human  rights  and  the  rights  of  each  child,  pupil/student  and  adult  and  respect  of  human dignity;  education  and  upbringing  in  democratically  organized  and  socially  responsible  institutions  in  which  openness, cooperation, tolerance, awareness of cultural and civilisational connections in the world, dedication to basic ethical values, the values of justice, truth, solidarity, freedom, honesty and responsibility are fostered and in which full respect of the rights of the child, pupils/students and adults are ensured. Democracy through inclusion of all the pupils/students in the system of education and upbringing is also among the tasks of the schools. Among the objectives of education and upbringing (Article 8), provision of  stimulating  and  safe  environments  for  integral  development  of  a  child,  pupil/student and  adult  is  included,  along  with the development  of  non-violent  conduct  and  the  zero  tolerance  to  violence,  development  of  awareness  of  the  importance  of sustainable  development,  protection  and  conservation  of  nature  and  the  environment  and  ecological  ethics,  protection  and wellbeing  of  animals,  development  of  positive  human  values,  development  of  the  sense  of  solidarity,  understanding  and constructive cooperation with others and nurturing companionship and friendship, development of competences for understanding  and  respecting  the  children's  rights,  human  rights,  civil  freedoms  and  capabilities  for  life  in  a  democraticall y organized and just society, development and respect of the racial, national, cultural, language, religious, gender, sexual and agerelated equality, tolerance and respect of diversity. The key competences for the life-long learning are (Article 11): the social and civic  competences,  while  among  the  general  interdisciplinary  competences  (Article  12)  is  the  responsible  participation  in democratic  society.  The  rights  of  the  child  and  of  the  pupils/students  (Article  79)  shall  be  exercised  through  respect  for  the personality, protection from discrimination, violence, abuse and neglect, information on rights and obligations, protection and fair  treatment  of  the  child  and  of  the  pupil/student  by  the  institution.  In  addition  to  that,  the  Law  defines  prohibition  of discrimination  (Article  110): 'Discrimination  and  discriminatory  behaviour,  having  the  purpose  of  making  any  unjustifiable distinction or providing unequal treatment and/or omission to act (exclusion, restriction or preferential treatment) in relation to a person or groups of persons, as well as in relation to the members of their families or persons who are close to them, either directly or indirectly, in an open or covert manner, which is based on race, colour of the skin, ancestors, citizenship, migrant and/or displaced person's status, nationality or ethnic origin, language, religious or political convictions, sex, gender ide ntity, sexual orientation, financial standing, social and cultural origin, birth, genetic characteristics, health condition, developmental impairments and disabilities,  marital  and  family  status,  criminal  record,  age,  appearance,  membership  in  political,  union  and other organisations and other actual and/or presumed personal characteristics, as well as on other bases, laid down by the law prescribing nondiscrimination and shall be prohibited in institutions'. The special measures introduced to achieve full equality, protection and progress of persons and/or groups of persons in an unequal position shall not be considered discrimination. The institutions shall be obliged to take all the measures prescribed by that Law in cases of suspected or determined discriminatory behaviour  in  the  institution.'  Article 112  prohibits  any  behaviour  having  the  purpose  of  insulting  the  reputation,  honour  or dignity. In addition to that, the LFSEU defines the support measures for the inclusion of children in the educational system and increasing the coverage by education. For example, Article 136 of the LFSEU prescribes that the pedagogical assistants shall provide assistance and additional support to the groups of children and pupils in institutions, in accordance with their needs, and assistance  to  the  staff  members  with  the  aim  of  improving  their  work.  In  their  work,  the  pedagogical  assistants  shall  realise cooperation  with  parents  and/or  with  other  legal  representatives,  authorities  of  the  local  self-government  units,  competent institutions, organisations and associations. The andragogical assistants shall provide support to adults aimed at their inclusion in the system of education, as well as assistance to the staff members in the realisation of educational programs. In their work, the andragogical assistants shall realise cooperation with the authorities of the local self-government units, competent institutions, organisations  and  associations.  With  the  aim  of  providing  clear  definitions  of  the  roles  of  the  pedagogical  and  andragogical assistants,  the  ME  has  passed  the  Rulebook  on  pedagogical  and  andragogical  assistants  (Official  Gazette  of  the  RS,  number 87/19), which has regulated the job description, the professional qualifications/education, additional knowledge/examinations/professional experience of pedagogical assistants. The most important effects of the support measures taken are reflected in the increased coverage of the Roma children and pupils/students and of the children and pupils/students with developmental impairments and disabilities by the system of education and upbringing, in the reduced  dropping out and early  leaving  of  school.  In  particular,  an  increase  is  observable  of  the  number  of  students  who  are  enrolled  in  and  who  are completing the secondary schools. The Law on Preschool Upbringing and Education (Official Gazette of the RS, No. 18/10, 101/17, 113/17, 10/19, 129/21), among the principles, includes the availability: of the equal right to and availability of all the forms  of  preschool  upbringing  and  education,  without  any  discrimination  or  exclusion  based  on  sex,  social,  cultural,  ethnic, religious  or  other  affiliation,  place  of  domicile  and/or  residence,  financial  standing  or  health  condition,  difficulties  and impairments in development and disabilities or on any other bases, the democracy: respect for the needs and rights of the child and  of  the  family,  including  the  right  to  respect  of  opinion,  active  participation,  decision-making  and  undertaking  of responsibility.  Article  14  defines that  a  child  who  is  a  foreign  national  and  a  stateless  child  and  a  child  who  is  a  citizenship seeker, a child from a vulnerable group without proof of residence or other personal identification documents, a refugee or a displaced person shall be enrolled in a preschool institution and/or in a school, which realizes the preparatory preschool program, under the same conditions and in the same manner as that prescribed for the nationals  of  the  Republic of  Serbia.  That  Law additionally defines support to children from vulnerable social groups (in Article 34) which shall be provided by the preschool institution that shall ensure removal of any physical obstacles and obstacles in communication, and/or provide adjustments and adaptation to the child who, due to social deprivation, developmental impairments, disability and other reasons, needs additional support in upbringing and education through production, passing and realisation of an individual educational plan (IEP1). In Article 4 of the Law on Primary Education and Upbringing (Official Gazette of the RS, No. 55/13, 101/17, 27/18, 10/19, 129/21, 129/21, 92/23), the right to primary education and upbringing is defined as the right of each person to free of charge and quality primary education and upbringing in public schools. Pupils of public schools may use, free of charge, the books, the school materials, transportation, meals, as well as accommodation, where necessary. The openness and flexibility of the educational system in the Republic of Serbia have been demonstrated again upon the onset of the migrant crisis. ##### Digital Education

Digital education is a term that refers to two different but complementary areas of educational policy - one includes measures aimed at the digital competence of teachers and students , while the other includes the pedagogical application of digital technologies in order to improve the quality of teaching and learning.

The  approach  to  developing  digital  education  is  comprehensive,  aligning  with  the priorities and objectives set forth in both Serbia's Strategy for Education Development  by  2030  and  the  European  Commission's  Action  Plan  for  Digital Education 2021-2027.

Key pillars of long-term development, among other measures, include:

- Strengthening IT infrastructure.

- Establishing an Education Management Information System for better monitoring and management.

-Increasing the number of schools with digital segments in their School Development Plans.

- Enabling more schools to offer high-quality blended teaching.

-Enhancing students' and teachers digital competencies.

Digital  education  has  the  potential  to  improve  quality  of  teaching  and  learning, particularly if it is focused on the use of the relevant Learning Management System

Unlike Emergency Remote Education, which is a temporary solution, blended learning aims to enhance everyday teaching and learning through consistent online support, becoming an integral part of traditional classroom instruction.

Additionally, the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in education aims to enhance personalized learning, streamline administrative tasks, and improve educational outcomes, while also rigorously examining its potential to ensure both effective and ethical implementation.

##### Digital School

Schools are strongly advised to conduct a SELFIE self-assessment every two years, discuss  the  results  to  identify  successful  practices  and  areas  needing  improvement, explore ways to apply digital technologies to enhance teaching and learning, define the Digital  component  of  their  School  Development  Plan ,  and  continuously monitor and adapt to technological changes.

Since  2017, EU  Commission's  SELFIE  tool for  self-assessing  schools'  digital capacities has been used in the Republic of Serbia. Currently, 80% of primary and secondary  schools  have  done  Selfie  at  least  once  and  are  aware  of  their  digital potential.

##### Digital Competences of Students

The  Law  on  Foundations  of  the  Education  System  in  Serbia  outlines  formal expectations for developing digital competences of students, emphasizing a comprehensive approach to media, information, and digital literacy. Media Literacy and Digital Competency are identified as key interdisciplinary competences, meant to be developed across all subjects through dynamic teaching methods. The law aims to shift  focus  from  mere  declarative  knowledge  to  practical  skills  and  attitudes  that enable  students  to  navigate  complex  social  situations.  Additionally,  ICT  skills, computational  thinking,  and  information  literacy  are  fostered  through  subjects  like Digital  World  and  Computer  Science,  as  well  as  through  project-based  learning integrated into all curricula.

Digital  World is  a  mandatory  school  subject  within  the  first  cycle  of  primary education, involving pupils aged 7-10. Implementation started from the school year 2020/21.  As  stated  in  the  curriculum,  the  overall  goal  of  teaching  and  learning  in Digital World is to develop students' digital competences in order to enable them to safely and correctly use digital devices for learning, communication, cooperation and the development of algorithmic thinking.

Digital  World  curricula  has  been  developed  for  the  first,  second,  third  and  fourth grade  of  the  primary  education.  The  subject  has  36  school  hours  per  year  and  is structured around three teaching areas: digital society, safe use of digital devices and computational thinking.

The  Ministry  also  encourages  the  use  of Open Educational Resources (OERs) to enhance teaching and learning.
