Responsibility for school funding in Australia is shared. State and territory governments provide most of the public funding for government schools, which they own and operate. The Australian Government provides most of the public funding for non government schools. Australian Government funding for all schools contributes to inclusion in education for all. Australian Government recurrent funding for schools is calculated based on the Schooling Resource Standard (SRS), which is a needs-based mechanism prescribed in the Australian Education Act 2013 for the calculation and distribution of Australian Government funding for schools. A school's total SRS includes a base amount for each primary and secondary student and up to 6 additional funding loadings for priority equity cohorts and disadvantaged schools. In 2024, the Australian Government funds at least 20% of each government's school SRS and 80% of each non government school's SRS. Students attending government schools are not required to pay tuition fees. However, parent contributions for certain non-tuition expenses such as materials, excursions and extra curricula activities are compulsory in some states and territories. In jurisdictions with compulsory contributions, financial assistance for parents unable to afford payment is available. Non-government schools set compulsory fees in consultation with their school community. Because its funding is based on enrolments in each sector, the students of all parents wishing to access school education where a compulsory tuition fee is not charged will attract Australian Government funding. The Australian Government delivers a range of supports to help families access the Early Childhood Education and Care (ECEC) they need through various Family Assistance Law, however, participation in ECEC is a voluntary decision of the parents or primary care-givers of the child. In the year before fulltime compulsory schooling begins, the Preschool Reform Agreement (PRA) between the Australian Government and all state and territory governments is in force. The objective of the PRA is to facilitate children's early learning and development and transition to school by: · maintaining universal access to affordable, · quality preschool programs for all children, · improving participation in preschool programs, · maximising the benefit of the preschool year by improving outcomes for children. There is no Commonwealth legislation explicitly enshrining the right to pre-primary education, and school attendance is not compulsory for children below the age of six.

The Australian Government has multiple policies and programs to support universal access to education. One key example is through the Community Child Care Fund (CCCF) Program, which supports eligible ECEC services to address barriers to participation in early childhood education and care in disadvantaged, regional and remote communities. In addition, the In Home Care (IHC) Program provides access to government subsidised ECEC in the family home and is targeted to assist parents or carers who are unable to access other approved ECEC options such as those who work non standard hours, are geographically isolated or families with challenging and complex needs. The Inclusion Support Program supports to eligible ECEC services to build their capacity and capability to include children with additional needs, so all children have genuine opportunities to access, participate and achieve positive learning outcomes. The program plays a vital role in ensuring children with additional needs, challenging and trauma related behaviours and serious medical or health conditions (including mental health) are included in ECEC services alongside their typically developing peers. The Australian Government also commissioned the Productivity Commission (PC) the Government's principa l review and advisory body on policies affecting the welfare of Australians -to undertake a comprehensive review of Australia's ECEC system. The PC final's report, released on 18 September 2024, is a significant input into the Government's work to chart the course to a universal ECEC system. The report considers how to build a high-quality early learning system that is affordable, accessible and inclusive, and is currently being considered by Government.

They build culturally safe and secure environments for all children and their families.'
