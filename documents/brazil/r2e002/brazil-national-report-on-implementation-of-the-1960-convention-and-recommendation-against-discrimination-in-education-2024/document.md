# Brazil  -  National report on the implementation of the 1960 UNESCO Convention and Recommendation against Discrimination in Education - 2024

Brazil ratified the 1960 Convention and Recommendation against Discrimination in Education on September 6, 1968, by means of Decree Nº 63.223. The prohibition of discrimination in education is enshrined in the Federal Constitution, both as a fundamental right (article 5) and a specific disposition in the education sector (articles 6, 23, 205, 206, 207, 208, 209, 210, 214, 225 among others). It is also a fundamental tenet of the “Law of Lines of Direction and Bases of the Education” (LDBEN — Law. No. 9.394/1996).

During the reporting period, the main activities undertaken by the Ministry of Education in order to advance the implementation of the 1960 Convention addressed important challenges in  Rural  Education,  Environmental  Education,  Indigenous  School  Education,  Ethnic-Racial Education  and  Quilombola  School  Education,  Special  Education  from  the  Perspective  of Inclusive  Education,  Bilingual  Deaf  Education,  Literacy  and  Adult  Education,  and  Human Rights Education.

Rural Education , aimed at Basic Education, seeks to guarantee the right to education for rural, riverine and forest peoples, covering all stages and modalities of Basic Education, developed from  curricular  proposals,  pedagogical  work  organization,  and  methodologies  and  school calendar adapted to the sociocultural and environmental reality of rural communities. This set of actions is linked to the National Program for Rural Education (PRONACAMPO  - MEC Ordinance No. 86/2013). Also noteworthy as important implementation mechanisms are the Operational  Guidelines  for  Basic  Education  in  Rural  Schools  (Resolution  CNE/CEB  No. 1/2002)  and  the  LDBEN  and  its  amendment,  which  includes  an  article  on  Alternating Pedagogy, establishing curricular contents and methodologies appropriate to the needs of rural school  students  (Law  No.  14.767/2023).  Regarding Environmental  Education ,  which  is enshrined in the Federal Constitution (Article 225, paragraph VI) and in national legislation (Law 9795/1999 and corresponding regulations establishing the National Policy for Environmental Education) the Coordination-General of Environmental Education for Diversity and Sustainability (CGAMS), at the ministry of Education, is currently engaged in reviewing the  Environmental Education policy in order to ensure that needs  and views of historically discriminated groups are fully included.

In the field of Indigenous School Education, the Directorate of Policies for Indigenous School Education at the Ministry of Education is engaged in promoting the rights and guarantees of ethnic minorities, including indigenous peoples, as established by the National Curriculum Guidelines for Indigenous Education in Basic Education (Resolution CNE/CEB No. 5, June 2012), and by the Decree 6.861, of May 2009, which regulates Indigenous School Education, and defines its organization in Ethnoeducational Territories (TEEs), with the participation of indigenous peoples, respecting their territoriality and specific social, historical, cultural, environmental, and linguistic needs. In this context, the following programs stand out: Support Program for Higher Education - Indigenous Intercultural Licensure and Pedagogy (PROLIND), Indigenous Knowledge Action in School (ASIE), and the National Program for Training

With a focus on Higher Education, the Directorate highlights the Permanence Scholarship for indigenous students, the Institutional Program for Initiation to Teaching (PIBID), and the creation of an Indigenous University. Finally, the National Commission for the Evaluation and Support of the Production of Indigenous Didactic and Literary Material (CAPEMA) currently promotes the production, evaluation, editing, and distribution of bilingual and multilingual indigenous educational materials.

The Directorate of Policies for Ethnic-Racial Education and Quilombola School Education at the Ministry of Education is currently working to implement the National Equity Policy, Education for Ethnic-Racial Relations, and Quilombola School Education (PNEERQ), established by MEC Ordinance No. 470, of May 14, 2024, aimed at promoting educational equity and inclusion, as established by LDBEN, and its modification (Law No. 11.645/2008), which made the inclusion of the teaching of African-Brazilian and Indigenous history and culture mandatory in school curricula. In this sense, the PNEEQR operates through the following structuring axes: 1) governance and strengthening of educational networks and collaboration regime; 2) diagnosis and monitoring of the implementation of Law No. 10.639/2003; 3) training of education professionals; 4) didactic, paradidactic, and pedagogical material; 5) protocols for identifying and responding to racism in education; 6) affirmation of quilombola trajectories; and 7) dissemination of knowledge.

The  Directorate  of  Policies  for Special  Education  from  the  Perspective  of  Inclusive Education at the Ministry of Education (DIPEPI) highlights that in 2023, the National Network Program  for  Continuing  Teacher  Training  offered  38  courses  in  various  areas  of  Special Education, as well as two projects focused on themes of Special Education from an Inclusive Perspective. Additionally, DIPEPI also promoted meetings of the Brazilian Braille Commission (CBB),  which  aims  to  develop  and  disseminate  the  Braille  System  in  all  its  applications. Moreover, the National Commission for Special Education from the Perspective of Inclusive Education (CNEEPEI), a consultative commission, established by Ordinance No. 996, of May 23, 2023, supports MEC in the elaboration, monitoring, and evaluation of the Special Education Policy from the Perspective of Inclusive Education. Regarding the challenges of implementing policies, DIPEPI emphasizes the need for full participation of federal entities in public inclusion policies, in order to qualify and expand the coverage of continuing education on the axes of attending to the specificities of students in Special Education.

In  the  field  of Bilingual  Deaf  Education ,  the  Directorate  of  Policies  for  Bilingual  Deaf Education (DIPEBS) at MEC is actively engaged in promoting the right to education in their first language for the deaf, based on Law 10.436/2002, which recognizes the Brazilian Sign Language,  and  Decree  5.626/2005,  which  regulates  the  aforementioned  law;  and  Law 13.146/2015, which guarantees the provision of bilingual education in Libras. In May 2023, the National Commission for Bilingual Deaf Education (CNEBS) was established, as an advisory and consultative body to support MEC in the elaboration, monitoring, and evaluation of the Bilingual Deaf Education Policy. Regarding teacher training, DIPEBS conducted, during 2023, continuing  education  courses,  offering  over  4,000  places  for  teachers  and  bilingual  deaf education professionals. The Directorate of Policies for Literacy and Adult Education at MEC is currently implementing three programs with a particular focus on inclusion: the Support Program for Education Systems for Youth and Adult Education (PEJA); the Articulated Actions Plan (PAR); and the Program for Youth and Adult Education Integrated with Professional Education (EJA-Integrada - EPT). Additionally, the Directorate is currently engaged in the modeling of the National Pact for overcoming illiteracy and qualifying Youth and Adult Education, with a particular focus on including groups facing discrimination.

Finally,  the  Coordination-General  of Educational  Policies  in  Human  Rights (CGDH) emphasizes that the human rights education policy is being operationalized through actions to combat rights violations related to gender, refugees, and stateless persons. The main objective of  the  policy  is  to  contribute  to  the  consolidation  of  a  human  rights  culture  in  educational institutions, encouraging the inclusion of human rights education as a structuring axis of basic and higher education curricula in accordance with LDBEN. Launched in 2003 and concluded in 2006, the National Plan for Human Rights Education (PNEDH) constitutes a legal normative framework that guides public policies for Human Rights Education in all education modalities. Based on the PNEDH, the National Education Council (CNE), through Opinion CNE/CP No. 8/2012, and Resolution No. 1 of the Full Council, of May 30, 2012, established the National Curriculum  Guidelines  for  Human  Rights  Education,  which  considered  Human  Rights Education as one of the fundamental axes of the right to education, based on the principles of human dignity, equality of rights, recognition and appreciation of differences and diversities, secularism of the State, democracy, education, transversality, experience, and globality, and socio-environmental sustainability. The human rights education policy seeks to respond to the demands  of  social  groups  that  have  difficulty  accessing  and  staying  in  Basic  and  Higher Education. In this regard, in 2024, 7,560 places were opened for basic education professionals to  enroll  in  a  specific  training  programme  on  "Human  Rights  and  Diversity  Education: educating and educating for the construction of a society based on human rights (CDHD)," a partnership between MEC and the Federal University of Uberlândia (UFU).

Additionally, in partnership with the Observatory of Human Rights and School Violence (Obdove) of the Federal University of São Carlos (UFSCar), an agreement was signed to offer a training course on "Human Rights, Equity, and Promotion of Diversity in Schools: Multipliers in Focus (CDHD II)," with 10,000 places offered to public basic education professionals.
