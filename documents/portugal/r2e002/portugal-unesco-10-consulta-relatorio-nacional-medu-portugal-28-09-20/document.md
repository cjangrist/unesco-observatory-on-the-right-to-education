## Covid-19 context response /OR / Measures taken in the pandemic context

At the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic, as schools were closed for health safety purposes, the Ministry of Education adopted a set of broad-scope measures, designed to guarantee the Right  to  Education,  particularly  regarding  the  access  of  all  students  to  a  quality,  inclusive education.

From  a  Human  Rights  perspective,  the  measures  thereby  adopted  are  consistent  with  the principles and provisions of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC), namely with its articles 3, 6 and 29, and the child best interest has been given primary consideration.

Distance learning, as well as support resources, has been put in place, using channels such as websites,  special  education  platforms,  television,  radio,  etc.  The  Directorate-General  for Education, in collaboration with schools, State Television (RTP), and the Gulbenkian Foundation, launched  a  dedicated  initiative,  #EstudoEmCasa,  providing  educational  content  on  several subjects, daily, from 9am to 6pm, and running from March 8 to June 19 2020.

While distance learning is a method to be gradually developed and implemented through the required  technological  means,  public  schools  have  been  provided  with  adequate  equipment following a needs assessment. In partnership with municipalities and civil society, IT equipment has been allocated to thousands of students. At present, a plan is under way to ensure all students computer and internet access, as from the opening of the next school year.

As building an inclusive school implies looking at the specifics of each and every student, DecreeLaw 54/2018 has updated the inclusive education legal framework, to ensure that no one is left behind. In the context of the current pandemic, non-discrimination strategies have been adopted by  the  schools,  to  specifically  care  for  disadvantaged  children,  and  children  from  vulnerable communities, as well as to facilitate community interaction, school-family relationship, and also to explore new learning ways.

## 3.6. Quality education

The right to education, and to an education that meets the needs of citizens and society, is one of  the  fundamental  cornerstones  of  community  development  and  an  essential  aspect  of Portuguese democracy.

Access to universal schooling from the age of 3 and compulsory education for 12 years means ensuring everyone has access to a system that responds to every person's needs and ensures their success.

School autonomy is one of the cornerstones of the curriculum flexibility programme and the work undertaken by school management bodies. As such, it is essential to ensure that schools can

In building a 21st-century curriculum, the Ministry of Education has adopted measures to provide quality learning that meets the needs of all students. To this end, Decree-Law No. 55/2018, 6th July, defines the organizing principles of primary and secondary school curricula, granting schools the autonomy to make the national curriculum more flexible, while Decree-Law No. 54/2018, 6th July, establishes the legal framework for inclusive education, with amendments introduced by Law no 116/2019, of 3rd September.

In this context, the aim is to support schools via autonomous and flexible curriculum management as  an  instrument  that  they  can  develop  locally,  in  discussion  with  students,  families  and  the community, so that all students acquire the competences contained in the exit profile for students leaving compulsory schooling. Knowing that autonomy is only effective if it is focused on the curriculum, schools can manage between 0% and 25% of the national curriculum, according to their target audience and situations.

In order to develop curricular, pedagogical or other innovation plans, schools were allowed to manage over 25% of the basic curriculum models for education and training provision - after the pedagogical innovation pilot projects (PPIPs) had shown schools' ability to implement innovative solutions to reduce dropout and school failure. Ordinance No. 181/2019 of 11th June defines the terms and conditions under which schools can implement management of over 25% of the basic curriculum models for educational and training provision in primary and secondary education, with a view to developing innovation plans.

In order to support schools and teachers in this process, the Ministry of Education has created a monitoring structure for the implementation of the abovementioned educational policy measures. This  support  intends  to  promote  networks  for  sharing  knowledge  and  pedagogical  and organizational practices that improve learning quality and teachers' professional development.

Portugal has been developing an education policy that seeks to intervene positively in schools inserted in Priority Intervention Educational Areas (TEIP schools), where there are students from more  vulnerable  contexts,  in  order  to  promote  a  quality  education  for  all,  improving  learning outcomes, reducing retention and early school leaving.

The TEIP program, regulated by Legislative Order No. 20/2012, of 3rd  October, has enabled the 137  school  clusters  that  integrate  these  territories  to  have  the  necessary  conditions  for  the promotion  of  the  education  success  of  all  students  by  means  of  strengthening  the  schools' autonomy,  with  the  implementation  of  their  own  projects  in  line  with  their  realties  and  local contexts.

The Directorate-General for Education, as the coordinating organism of the program, established new guidelines in 2018, so that, in the scope of the new legal framework for curriculum autonomy

## 3.8. Respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms

With regard to the full development of the human personality and strengthening fundamental rights and freedoms through education, one should mention two of the organizational principles of  the  Portuguese  educational  system,  as  enshrined  in  the  Comprehensive  Law  on  the Educational  System  (CLES,  1986):  'Contribute  to  the  pupil's  fulfillment  through  complete development  of  personality,  character  formation  and  citizenship  (…)'  and  'Contribute  to developing democratic spirit and practice by adopting participatory structures and processes for defining education policy, administering and running the school system and daily pedagogical experience (…)' - respectively paragraphs b) and l) of Article 3 rd of the CLES.

But also, a special reference should be made to the anticipation of a distinctive feature of the present  curricular  framework,  in  the  form  of  an  explicit  naming  of  possible  components  of  a «domain of personal and social training», which could comprehend, according to the Parliament, in 1986, «ecological education, consumer education, family education, sexual education, accident

In fact, the recently approved Decree-Law on curriculum for the basic and secondary education (Decree-Law  no.  55/2018,  6 th July)  consecrates  the  mandatory  creation  of  school-based strategies for the implementing of a specific curricular component, Citizenship and Development ,  aimed at developing a broad range of active citizenship competences deemed essential  for  any  young  person  to  achieve  until  he/she  reaches  18.  This  shows  the  special commitment  of  the  Portuguese  authorities  on  reaching  the  target  4.7  of  the Sustainable Development Goals long before 2030, as far as it concerns formal education institutions.

The above mentioned renewal of the national curriculum for the basic and secondary education followed two major guidelines:

- the National Strategy for Citizenship Education (2017), which draws the main lines according to which Citizenship Education , through the curricular component Citizenship and Development , is  to  be  implemented  within  the  national  curriculum  (either  as  an  autonomous subject or through a transversal and interdisciplinary approach), with seventeen (plus  'others') citizenship domains among which to choose, although some must be dealt with throughout the entire compulsory schooling cycle (12 years, from primary to upper secondary education);

-  the Students'  Profile  by  the  end  of  Compulsory  Schooling (2017) , conceived  with  the technical  involvement  of  several  bodies  and  individuals,  both  national  and  international,  and according to which the syllabuses have subsequently been object of a global reorientation, in order to achieve a core of learning contents and goals core curriculum competences - for each subject, consistent with the Profile .

Consequently, as regards Education for Citizenship, understood as an integral part of the right to education - and aimed at promoting the respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms -, the current curricular framework (Decree-Law no. 55/2018) introduces some major changes:

§ the creation of the curricular component Citizenship and Development ;

§ the obligation, for each school or school cluster, to create and approve its own Strategy for  Citizenship  Education,  combining  the  guidelines  of  the  National  Strategy  and  the mandatory  dispositions  of  the  Decree-Law  no.  55/2018  with  their  own  educational projects;

§ the impulse to networking, through local and regional partnerships with local authorities, NGOs, corporations, etc. - again, within the framework of the school's own Strategy;

§ the renewed focus on the individual student as an increasingly free and responsible agent whose autonomy is built upon a 'broad set of knowledge, skills, attitudes and values in action' (to quote an influential position paper issued within the project OECD Education 2030 );

§ the preference for a 'whole school approach', not only in Citizenship and Development but for the entire curriculum development.

According to the National Strategy for Citizenship Education the  areas  chosen  should  (1) express a «non-abstract conception of citizenship», (2) «identify […] key areas throughout the school curriculum» and (3) «identify […] the essential skills of citizen education (skills for a Culture of Democracy).» In this way, «the approach to citizenship education should focus on […] three axes […]» through which an active citizenship is to be built: «Individual  civic  attitude  (citizen identity, individual autonomy, human rights); Interpersonal relations (communication, dialogue); Social and intercultural relations (democracy, sustainable human development, globalization and interdependence, peace and the management of conflicts).»

In  the  meantime,  and  following  the  content  guidelines,  which  were  set  both  in  a  previous document Education for Citizenship - Guidelines , published in December 2012 and updated in November 2013 - and later in the National Strategy for Citizenship Education - from 2017 onwards -,  several Reference documents have also been published, or are being drafted - as already stated in the 9 th consultation report (2012-2015) - and it is now expected that all areas will be covered through the next school year, and online resources are available at a specific website, at https://cidadania.dge.mec.pt/ .  However, these documents are just an optional support for schools and teachers, not textbooks or syllabuses.

Since there have been no changes in rules and regulations concerning school's administration and management, as well as in the Statute of the Student, the remarks made in the previous report are to be restated. In fact, the right of pupils to participate in school life is visible through the representation of students in the General Council and in the Class Councils. The students' right to participate in school life and have a voice on the issues that affect them is also exercised through their class 'delegates' (elected by the students), the Council of Class Delegates and the Students' Assemblies, in accordance to the respective School Internal Regulation (drafted with the participation of the students), as well as through Students' Associations. The student's right to participate in the school life is effective in practice, and is not restricted to the mechanisms of representation  provided  for  by  the  relevant  legal  instruments  (namely,  the  Regulation  of Autonomy, Administration and Management of Public Establishments of Pre- 2 nd School, Basic and Secondary Education - Decree-Law no. 137/2012, of 2 nd July- and the Statute of the Student and School Ethics - Law no. 51/2012, of 5 th September)

Since 2017 the initiative Students' Voice is in place in order to generate in all public school spaces for collecting and discussing students' perspectives, especially regarding education priorities and human  rights,  and  to  make  these  contributions  visible  in  national  events  with  government members and high-level public administration officers.

For instance, the 70 th anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the 40 th anniversary of the ratifying of the European Convention on Human Rights Convention (December 10, 2018), were celebrated with a national one day long initiative presided by the Minister of Education, connecting all the Portuguese schools and school clusters, via internet, with a large and effective participation of students, who discussed and presented several topics concerning human rights protection and promotion.

Also, during the International Conference Education, Citizenship, World (Lisbon, 28-29 May 2019) took place another Students' Voice Debate moderated by the State Secretary for Education and focused on topics of the Conference. Organized by the General Directorate of Education ( DireçãoGeral de Educação - DGE) in partnership with the Council of Europe, the Portuguese Speaking Countries Community and the Organization of the Ibero-American States for Education, Science and  Culture,  the  Conference  was  attended  by  more  than  100  participants  (decision-makers, teachers, educational experts and students from several countries).
