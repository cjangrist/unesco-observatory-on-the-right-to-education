Cabinet of the Secretary of State for Education
# Legislative order no.  5908/2017

The 21st Constitutional Government recognises education as a privileged means of fostering social  justice  and  equal  opportunities,  being  one  of  the  principles  that  shape  its  policy  to promote  a  quality  and  successful  education  for  all  students  throughout  the  12  years  of compulsory schooling.

Promoting a quality education implies ensuring that school achievement means effective and meaningful learning, with consolidated knowledge to be mobilised in concrete situations that foster the development of high level competences, which, in turn, contribute to a successful citizenship  within  the  context  of  the  challenges  posed  by  contemporary  society.  The  set  of competences outlined in  the Students'  Profile  by  the  End  of  Compulsory  Schooling and  that have been presented in Portugal as well as in the most varied international forums embodies transversal,  transdisciplinary  competences  in  a  web  that  interrelates  and  mobilises  a  solid combination of knowledge, skills, attitudes and values. The successful citizen is informed, but he is also able to integrate knowledge, solve problems, master different scientific and technical languages, he cooperates, he is autonomous, he has aesthetic and artistic sensitivity and takes care of his well-being.

The  implementation  of  the  competence  profile  that  has  just  been  mentioned  implies intentionality  and  educational  action  leading  to  the  development  of  the  planned  areas.  As such, being aware that pedagogic differentiation is one of the main tools to guarantee  better learning, it is critical that schools are provided with the necessary conditions that allow them to manage the curriculum in order to integrate strategies so as to promote  better learning within specific contexts to meet the needs of different students.

To fulfil this goal of promoting better learning leading to the development of higher-level competences, the Program of the Government comprises guidelines for the implementation of an educational policy that, assuming the centrality of schools, of their students and teachers, enables a flexible and contextualised management of the curriculum, acknowledging that the effective exercise of autonomy in education is only fully guaranteed if the object of such autonomy is the curriculum.

Traditionally,  the  tools  for  school  autonomy  do  not  include  the  core  action  area  of  schools, that is, the autonomy within curriculum development.

To provide schools with the opportunity to participate in curriculum development, establishing priorities within the contextualised appropriation of the curriculum and assuming diversity by finding  the  options  that  best  respond  to  the  challenges  of  their  educational  project,  is  to sustain  education  in  the  combination  of  three  fundamental  elements:  autonomy,  trust  and responsibility - autonomy based on trust in each school, while being self-aware of its reality and of the responsibility of providing a public service on behalf of a  quality education.

## CHAPTER I
### General provisions
#### Article 1
##### Object

The  Project  for  Autonomy  and  Curriculum  Flexibility,  based  on  a  pedagogical  experience, defines the guiding principles and rules for the design, implementation and evaluation of the curriculum for primary and secondary education, in order to achieve the 'Students' Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling'.

#### Article 2
##### Definitions

a) “Curriculum for primary and secondary education” means the set of knowledge, skills and attitudes comprised in curriculum reference documents, in particular in the “Core Curriculum Competences”, from which schools define their curriculum choices in order to achieve the set of competences outlined in the “Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling”;

b) “Core Curriculum Competences”, the common set of knowledge to be attained, that is, the contents of structured, indispensable, aligned and meaningful disciplinary knowledge, as well as the skills and attitudes to be developed by all students in each subject or discipline area generally per year of schooling.

c) “Curriculum reference documents”, the set of documents that embody the knowledge to be acquired, the skills and attitudes to be developed by the students, namely the syllabi, curriculum outcomes, guidelines, professional profiles and references for the National Qualifications Catalogue, as well as the “Core Curriculum Competences” for each subject and/or discipline area, the latter being the core curriculum guidelines for the planning, implementation and assessment of teaching and learning.

d) “Curriculum matrices” are the set of disciplinary areas and subjects to be taught per cycle of studies and year of schooling or per training cycle (the latter for vocational courses), as well as the expected instruction time for each of them, which supports the development of the curriculum in curriculum planning tools, at school and in the classroom;

e) “Autonomy and curriculum flexibility” is the possibility provided to schools so as to manage the primary and secondary education curriculum and the organisation of

curriculum matrices based on the subjects, disciplinary areas and their instruction time, based on the possibility of enriching the curriculum with the knowledge, skills and attitudes that contribute to the achievement of the competences provided for in the “Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling”.

f) “Domains of curriculum autonomy”, areas of interdisciplinary work and / or cross curriculum alignment, comprised in the curriculum matrix of an educational and training offer, as a result of the local implementation and management of curriculum carried out by each school within the curriculum planning.

#### Article 3

##### Guiding principles

The design, implementation  and evaluation of the primary and secondary education curriculum, in the scope of the current project, are in line with the following guiding principles:

a) Fostering the improvement of teaching and learning quality so that all students are able to achieve the Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling;

b) Implementation of an effective exercise of curriculum autonomy, enabling schools to identify effective curriculum options and in line with the specificity of the educational project and the strategic action plan or, in the case of schools in the Priority Intervention Education Territories, the multiannual improvement plan;

c) Guaranteeing an inclusive school, whose diversity, flexibility, innovation and personalisation meet the students’ heterogeneity, eliminating barriers to access the curriculum and the learning process in line with the Students’ Profile;

d) Fostering a better alignment of the three cycles of studies/levels of education (primary and lower secondary education) and upper secondary education, assuming an integrated and sequential curriculum management;

e) Mobilisation of education agents aiming at promoting the school achievement of all students, based on curriculum co-authoring and shared responsibility;

f) Valuing the management and interdisciplinary cross curriculum teaching through the development of projects that combine the cross curriculum competences for the different subjects, that are planned, implemented and evaluated by the teachers each class or year of schooling/grade;

g) Contextual flexibility in curriculum management using the methods, approaches and procedures that are most appropriate for all students to achieve the Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling;

h) Valuing the identity of secondary education as a level of education which offers students different school pathways that aim at addressing their vocational interests and allow

the achievement of compulsory schooling, labour market integration and the pursuit of further studies for all;

i) Designing an integrative curriculum that aggregates all school activities and projects, assuming them as a source of learning and competence development by students;

j) Assumption of the importance of the transdisciplinary nature of learning, mobilisation of different literacies and multiple theoretical and practical competences, promoting scientific knowledge, intellectual curiosity, critical and interventional spirit, creativity and collaborative work;

k) Valuing the Portuguese language and culture as vehicles for fostering national identity;

l) Valuing foreign languages as vehicles for fostering global and multicultural identity and facilitating access to information and technology;

m) Assumption of arts, science and technology, sport and humanities as structuring components of the curriculum matrix regarding the multiple educational and training offerings;

n) Promotion of citizenship and development education throughout all compulsory schooling;

o) Valuing collaborative and interdisciplinary work in the planning, implementation and assessment of learning;

p) Assessment of learning as an integral part of curriculum management and a tool for teaching and learning;

q) Fostering the regulatory purpose of external assessment, valuing a timely and rigorous intervention, supported by the information attained from the benchmarking process, in order to overcome difficulties in the different curriculum domains;

r) Valuing complementarity between internal and external assessment processes of learning;

s) Acknowledgement of the importance of external assessment and other specific assessment modalities that convene external entities for the purposes of certification and pursuit of studies by the end of primary and secondary education;

t) Students and parents’ involvement in the identification of school curriculum options;

u) Acknowledging teachers as key agents of curriculum development with a critical role regarding its evaluation, reflection on the options to be taken, their feasibility and suitability to the contexts of each school community;

## Chapter 2

### Primary and secondary education curriculum
### SECTION I
#### Conception/design
#### Article 4
##### Purpose

1 - The curriculum aims at ensuring that all students, regardless of their school pathway, achieve the competences defined in the Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling

2 - In order to achieve this purpose, the following principles underlie the design of the curriculum:
a) Access to the curriculum by all students within a framework of equal opportunities, based on the acknowledgement that all have learning and educational development skills;;
b) Consistency and sequentiality of learning;
c) Possibility of a change of direction or reguidance regarding school pathways within the levels of education in which there are different education offerings and pathways;
d) Assumption of the projects and activities developed within the school community as an integral part of the curriculum;
e) Greater curriculum flexibility regarding the secondary education students, through the possibility of choosing subjects aligned according to their interest, building their own school pathway;
f) Curriculum enrichment by means of providing a school complementary offer component through the creation of new subjects in primary and lower secondary education, using the time credit defined in the Order that sets out the rules for the school year organisation;
g) Fostering moments of support for student learning;
h) Offering to all students the curriculum component Citizenship and Development;
i) Offering Portuguese as a Second Language for students with another mother tongue and Portuguese Sign Language for deaf students;
j) Development of programming within the learning of Information and Communication Technologies, with a possibility of beginning the study of programming in lower-primary education.
k) Curriculum Enrichment Activity offer in primary and lower secondary education on a playful, formative and cultural basis.

#### Article 6

##### Curriculum autonomy

1 - In the scope of curriculum autonomy and flexibility, schools can manage up to 25% of the weekly instruction time registered in the curriculum matrices per year of schooling or, in the case of vocational courses (upper primary and secondary education), the total instruction time for each training cycle.

2 - Taking into account the context of each school, domains of curriculum autonomy or new subjects can be created, regardless of the existence of the disciplinary areas and subjects foreseen in the curriculum matrices.

3 - The new subjects, created by each school within the instruction time for the Complementary Offer, are the ones that present their own curriculum documents and identity.

#### Article 13

##### Development of curriculum planning

1 - Considering the competence areas outlined in the Students’ Profile by the End of Compulsory Schooling, each school sets out priorities and takes options within the development of curriculum planning aiming at:
a) The enhancement of arts, science, sport, humanities, ICT and practical and experimental work, as well as the embeddedness of regional and local components;
b) The acquisition and development of multiple competences, such as research, assessment, reflection, critical and autonomous information mobilisation, aiming at solving problems and reinforcing students' self-esteem;
c) Fostering communication and expression experiences in Portuguese and foreign languages in spoken, written, visual and multimodal modalities;
d) Exercising active citizenship and social participation in contexts of sharing and collaboration and confronting ideas on current issues;
e) Implementing project-based learning as a dynamic centred on the role of students as authors fostering deeper learning.

2 - The curriculum options of each school comprise, among others, the following possibilities:
a) Partial or total combination of subjects;
b) Alternation, throughout the school year, of periods of disciplinary practice with periods of multidisciplinary functioning, by means of collaborative work;
c) Development of practical or experimental work splitting classes or using another organisation;
d) Including projects developed at school in blocks within the weekly instruction time, in a rotating or other suitable way;
e) Redistribution of the instruction time outlined for the different subjects in the curriculum matrices, fostering interdisciplinary working time and sharing instruction time between different subjects.
f) Organisation of the subjects functioning on a quarterly or semiannual basis, or other organisation;
g) Creating new subjects, spaces or working time for the development of components of local curriculum, among others, within an interdisciplinary approach.

5 - The class curriculum plannings /plans are developed, in lower primary education, by the generalist teacher after previous discussions in the teachers’ council, while in upper primary and lower secondary education it is developed after discussions in the teachers’ class council.

6 - The provisions of paragraphs 1 to 4 of this article shall apply, with due adaptations, to the curricular planning tools to be adopted by the school, in line with the provisions of article 14, paragraph 3

7 - The curriculum plans are submitted to the pedagogical council.

#### Article 17

##### Management of the class curriculum plan

In the work dynamics to be implemented, in the scope of the class curriculum plan, the class teacher, in liaison  with  the  teaching  council,  and  the  class  council,  coordinated  by  the  class director and / or course, should, as a rule, ensure:

a) A work of an interdisciplinary and disciplinary nature;
b) A preventive action, that allows to anticipate and to prevent failure and early school leave;
c) The adequacy, diversity and complementarity of teaching and learning strategies, as well as the assessment tools and educational resources to be adopted in the class;
d) Involvement of students in the planning, development and monitoring of the class curriculum plan;
e) The regular practice of monitoring the class curriculum plan, evaluating, in accordance with its intentionality, the impact of the strategies and measures adopted;
f) The production of descriptive information on student performance, promoting quality learning and self-regulation.

#### Article 18

##### Pedagogical practices

Aiming at an effective appropriation of knowledge, as well as the development of skills and attitudes by students, pedagogical practices should value, in particular:
a) The management of the horizontal alignment of the curriculum implemented by the generalist teacher, by the class responsible and / or course or by another teacher;
b) Explanation of the attained learning, expected performances and assessment tools, as references that constitute the specific learning profile of the students;
c) Knowledge mobilisation regarding situations and problems of daily life or the environment;
d) The organisation of teaching by providing activities for observation, experimentation and questioning of reality in an integrated way;
e) The development of cooperative learning activities;
(f) The critical use of information sources and the preference given to the use of information and communication technologies;
g) The promotion of activities of students’ civic intervention, privileging free initiative, autonomy and responsibility;
h) The creation of stimulating environments and enhancers of the development of intellectual curiosity.
i) The development of high level competences, focusing on research, analysis, synthesis, evaluation and communication activities.

#### Article 19

##### Measures to promote school achievement

1 - Within the framework of the strategic action plans and the multiannual improvement plans, schools adopt measures that aim at promoting school achievement, favouring, among others:
a) The promotion of practices that anticipate and prevent school failure and early school leave, through a focus on pedagogical differentiation and early intervention rather than on remedial strategies;
b) The involvement of students and parents in the definition, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of measures to promote educational achievement;

c) The empowerment of schools’ resources through programs that allow an intervention on the conditioning factors and predictors of educational achievement;

d) Periodic assessment of their action plans, in their multiple dimensions, focusing on the impact assessment of locally defined strategies and identified as relevant for the promotion of school achievement;

e) The collaborative work, valuing the exchange of knowledge and experiences, through practices of:
i) Coordination between teachers, various levels of education and various disciplinary areas;
ii) Temporary exchange between teachers of the same area or disciplinary domain.

f) The organisation of students in working groups aiming at the:
i) Acquisition, development and consolidation of specific learning, with a view to promoting the alignment between disciplinary areas, functioning as a rule, on a temporary basis;
ii) Providing support to learning, based on a methodology of integration of several disciplinary areas, privileging the research, treatment and selection of information;

g) The implementation of mentoring, aiming at the educational process guidance, namely through self-regulation of attained learning outcomes and the suitability to students’ academic and social expectations;

h) The promotion, through psychology and guidance services, of educational and vocational guidance actions, so that students may choose courses, areas and subjects that meet their vocational interests;

- 4  -  Formative  assessment,  defined  in  no.  2,  is  the  main  assessment  modality  and  allows  to obtain  privileged  and  systematic  information  within  the  different  curriculum  domains,  and should be the basis for  the learning support, namely to self-regulation within the students' school pathways, aligned with provided information for the parents.

#### Article 26

##### Assessment effects

1 - The diagnostic assessment enables the students’ school integration, it supports the definition of teaching strategies as well as school and vocational guidance.

b) In the mainstream courses and in the specialised artistic courses of upper secondary education, on the approval in each subject, the progression in the non-terminal subjects, the transition to the subsequent year of schooling or the reguidance of the students’ school pathway and the accomplishment of upper secondary education.
