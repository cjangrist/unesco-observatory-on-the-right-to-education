## A. Laws and policies (preamble and paras 4 and 7 of  the 1947 Recommendation )

### A1a .  Please  describe  any  specific  national  or  sub-national  laws,  legislation  or  legal framework (s) in your country that govern the promotion of global citizenship education (GCED) and/or education for sustainable  development  (ESD).  Explain  whether  GCED and/or ESD themes are mentioned explicitly or are inferred and what types or levels of education are covered.

- The National Education Law (2014) recognizes the right of all citizens to education, and in particular free, compulsory primary education. The law also mandates the establishment  of  an  education  quality  assurance  system;  extends  the  basic education system to 13 years (including a Kindergarten year); allows for the learning of nationalities' languages and culture, and the use of nationalities' languages as a classroom language; provides a definition of, as well as a commitment to, inclusive education and commits to a decentralized education system.

- The National Education law was promulgated in 2014 and amended in 2015' and Article  4  in  2015  amended  law  states  that:  (f)  Having  the  inclusive  education including special education scheme and special education services for those who are lack of any opportunity to learn for any reason including school age child, youth and the disabled in order to have right to education: and (g) Every citizen shall have the right to education and opportunities for life-long learning shall be created.

- The Basic Education Law was promulgated in 2019.

- The  Ministry  of  Education  (MOE)  has  undertaken  a  three-and-a-half-year Comprehensive  Education  Sector  Review  (CESR)  involving  three  phases:  a  rapid assessment  (Phase  1); in-depth  research  and  analysis of critical  sub-sector challenges (Phase 2); and drafting and building ownership for an evidence-based and costed National Education Strategic Plan (NESP) for the period 2016-21(Phase 3).  The  NESP  (2016-2021) has provided the government, education stakeholders and citizens with a 'roadmap' for sector-wide education reforms over the next five years that dramatically improve access to quality education for students at all levels of the national education system.

- By the end of the 2020-21 fiscal year the MOE has been committed to achieving the NESP goal statement: Improved teaching and learning, vocational education

### F1 Please describe any major initiatives that have been undertaken to promote GCED and/or ESD in technical and vocational education and training. Describe briefly the outcomes and impact of such initiatives and provide examples of good practices.

#### Implementation in Rakhine State by the Department of Technical and Vocational Education and Training

In  order  to  access  to  vocational  education,  the  Department  of  Technical  and Vocational  Education  and  Training  offers  the  formal  courses  for  local  people  in Rakhine State without the discrimination of ethnic/religion and gender. Courses in the field of Engineering as well as the short-term vocational courses are also offered at  the  Government  Technical  Institute  (Thandwe),  Government  Technical  Institute (Kyauk Phyu) and Government Technical High School (Sittway) to meet the needs of the region.

In Maungdaw, Motorcycle Repair Course was opened from (27-8-2018) to (7-9-2018) and (20) students joined the course. Basic Welding Course and Brick Laying Course were offered from (20-8-2018) to (31-8-2018) and a total of (40) students joined the course. In cooperation with Government Technical High School (Sittway) and NRC based in Norway, Basic Welding Course, Brick Laying Course and Household Wiring Course were offered at the Buthitaung starting from (2-9-2019) to (11-9-2019). A total student of 60 students attended the course. In Maungdaw, Basic Welding Course, Brick Laying Course and Household Wiring Course were offered from (16-9-2019) to (25-9-2019).
