## I. Ratification and applicability of the Convention against Discrimination in Education[1]

Is your country a State Party to the Convention?

No

Does your country plan to ratify this instrument?

No, not considering ratification.

- Please explain why:

The majority of the statements in the UNESCO Convention are not relevant anymore and do not meet more advanced legal regulation, currently in force in Lithuania, compared to that, which is established in the Convention, adopted in 1960. Both the EU and the Council of Europe, a member of which Lithuania is, raise higher requirements/standards, related to the implementation of the human rights and non-discrimination in education. Both the Lithuanian legal base and the existing international obligations currently ensure an adequate implementation of these rights and the principle of non-discrimination in education.

### 1. Towards an inclusive non-discriminatory education system

Please indicate whether the rights and principles laid out in the Convention/Recommendation informed the elaboration of the education legal and/or policy framework:

Yes

- If yes, please provide reference:

Article 41 of the Constitution establishes that education is compulsory for persons under the age of 16, education at state and municipal schools of general education, vocational schools, and schools of further education is free of charge. Higher education is accessible to everyone according to individual abilities. Well performing students shall be guaranteed education at state higher education institutions free of charge. The Law on Education of the Republic of Lithuania establishes the principles upon which the educational system is based, one of them is  equal opportunities - the educational system is socially fair, it ensures the implementation of person's rights, it guarantees the access to education for all, the attainment of a general education level and a primary qualification, and creates conditions to develop the acquired qualification or gain a new one. This law also establishes the right and duty of all residents to study, indicating that each citizen and foreigners, who has the right of permanent or temporary residence in the Republic of Lithuania, has the right to study, attain an education level and a qualification. The State takes measures that in Lithuania each child studies according to pre-primary, primary, basic and secondary education curricula. The State guarantees each citizen of the Republic of Lithuania and each foreigners, who has the right of permanent or temporary residence in the Republic of Lithuania, pre-primary, primary, basic and secondary education; as well as access to higher education study programmes or vocational training programmes that result in the acquisition of the first qualification. The Law on Vocational Education also establishes the principle of equal opportunities - the vocational education system is socially fair, it ensures equality among persons, regardless of their gender, race, nationality, language, origin, social status, faith, beliefs or attitudes; it guarantees the first qualification for each person, also creating conditions to develop the qualifications already attained or acquire a new one. The Law on Higher Education and Research establishes the principles of equal opportunities and academic ethics in the Lithuanian higher education and research system.

Article 5 of the Law on Education establishes the principles of the education system.

The principles of the education system are as follows:

Equality of opportunity - the education system is socially just, ensuring the realization of individual rights, providing access to education for everyone, acquiring general education and initial qualifications, and creating conditions for improving existing qualifications or acquiring new ones,

Contextuality - the education system is closely linked to the economic, social, and cultural context of the country's development, renewing itself along with it and meeting the constantly changing needs of society,

Efficiency - the education system aims for quality results, using available resources wisely and efficiently, constantly evaluating, analyzing, and planning its activities based on effective management - making appropriate and timely decisions,

Continuity - the education system is flexible, open, based on the interaction of various forms and institutions; it creates conditions for lifelong learning for everyone,

Inclusivity - the education system provides opportunities for everyone to develop, enhance their abilities and skills, receive necessary assistance, experience success in learning, social, cultural, and/or other activities, and not be discriminated against due to the diversity of learning needs and/or the need for educational assistance.

- Please elaborate and, where appropriate, indicate the legal and policy measures taken:

Article 5 of the Law on Education establishes the principles of the education system.

The principles of the education system are as follows:

Equality of opportunity - the education system is socially just, ensuring the realization of individual rights, providing access to education for everyone, acquiring general education and initial qualifications, and creating conditions for improving existing qualifications or acquiring new ones,

Inclusivity - the education system provides opportunities for everyone to develop, enhance their abilities and skills, receive necessary assistance, experience success in learning, social, cultural, and/or other activities, and not be discriminated against due to the diversity of learning needs and/or the need for educational assistance.

If the parents (guardians, caregivers) of a student with special educational needs wish, conditions are provided for the student to be educated closer to their place of residence in a preschool education institution, mainstream school, vocational training institution, or any state, municipal (regional) school designated for students with special educational needs. The school for the child is recommended by the pedagogical-psychological service.

The vocational training institution, higher education institution establishes the procedure for admission to these schools for individuals with special educational needs.

The municipality, in whose territory they reside, is responsible for the accessibility of education for students with special educational needs. Education accessibility is ensured by adapting the school environment, providing psychological, special pedagogical, special and social pedagogical assistance, supplying technical aids for education in the school, and special teaching aids, among other methods established by law.

Social inclusion for economically disadvantaged families, refugees, children not attending school, unemployed individuals, individuals returning from correctional facilities, as well as individuals undergoing treatment for alcoholism and drug addiction, and those who do not fit into society is ensured by providing them with social services and educational assistance. Targeted social and educational programs are implemented by the Government and municipalities directly or through schools. These programs can also be developed and implemented by non-governmental organizations and other legal and natural persons. State and municipal institutions, based on data from population registers and other state and informational registries, determine the number of children not attending school and their educational needs, and together with schools, implement targeted programs to involve these children in educational activities.

In Lithuania, there are four national consultation centres: the Lithuanian Centre for Inclusive Education, the Lithuanian Centre for Education of the Blind and Visually Impaired, the Lithuanian Centre for Education of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing, and the "Diemedžio" Education Centre. These centres invite teachers, school and kindergarten leaders, education support specialists, and teacher assistants to participate in a free of charge remote consultations, thematic seminars, and training sessions on the issues of inclusive education.

- Please elaborate and, where appropriate, indicate the legal and policy measures taken:

Every citizen of the Republic of Lithuania and a foreigner who has the right to reside permanently or temporarily in the Republic of Lithuania is guaranteed education in the state language and learning of the state language. The Law of Education establishes that in areas where a foreigner traditionally resides, if the community requests, the municipality provides education in the language of the national minority.

General education and non-formal education schools provide conditions for pupils belonging to national minorities to nurture their national, ethnic, and linguistic identity, to learn their native language, history, and culture. In general education and non-formal education schools, where, in accordance with the wishes of parents (guardians, caregivers) and pupils, education in the language of a national minority or education in the language of a national minority is legalized in the regulations, the educational process may be conducted, or some subjects may be taught in the language of a national minority. In these schools, the Lithuanian language subject is an integral part of the educational program, and no less time is allocated for its teaching than for teaching the native language. In such schools in pre-school and pre-primary education programs, no less than 5 hours per week are allocated for education in the Lithuanian language.

Primary, basic, and secondary education programs are implemented through bilingual education: in the language of a national minority and in the Lithuanian language. Lithuanian language is taught integrally in the primary education program, and in the basic and secondary education program - during those lessons when topics from Lithuanian history and geography, world cognition, and the basics of citizenship are covered;

At the request of parents (guardians, caregivers), other subjects of the primary, basic, and secondary education programs are taught in the Lithuanian language.

A person belonging to a national minority can learn their native language in a school conducting non-formal education programs or with another education provider.

Conditions are created for the children of a person who has the right to reside permanently or temporarily in the Republic of Lithuania to learn the state language, in the state language, and, if possible, to learn their native language.

In Lithuania, minors can start working from the age of 14, but depending on whether they fall into the children or adolescents age group, different restrictions apply to the nature of their work, working and rest time, as well as the formation of employment contracts. Even with a 14-year-old, work must be formalized through an employment contract, in which the parties agree on essential terms such as job functions, pay, workplace, work time norms, and work schedule. To form an employment contract for a child, written consent from one of the parents or another legal guardian, a medical certificate from a health care institution stating that the child is fit for the specific work, and written consent from the school during the academic year are required.

It is emphasized that children can only perform light duties that are within their physical capabilities and do not negatively impact their safety, health, physical, psychological, moral, or social development. Minors are also prohibited from working in any process involving alcohol (receiving orders, serving).

For young individuals, it's important to know their allowable working hours. During non-school periods, children can work up to 6 hours per day and 30 hours per week. During the school year, they can work up to 12 hours per week: up to 2 hours on school days and up to 6 hours on non-school days, if working during the term or semester, but not during school hours. Adolescents can work no more than 8 hours per day including daily class hours and no more than 40 hours per week including class hours.

Child labor is prohibited during these hours: from 8 PM to 6 AM, and in the morning from 6 AM to 7 AM before classes. There must be at least 2 rest days per week, consecutively if possible, with one being Sunday. Children's daily uninterrupted rest period over 24 hours must be no less than 14 hours. Adolescent work from 10 PM to 6 AM is prohibited. Adolescents must have at least 2 rest days per week, consecutively if possible, one of which should be Sunday.
