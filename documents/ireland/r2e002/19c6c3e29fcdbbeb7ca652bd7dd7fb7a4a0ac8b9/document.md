# Education and Training Integrated Reform Delivery Plan 2015

## Section 1 Governance

Robust  governance  arrangements  are  in  place  to  drive,  manage  and  monitor  implementation  of  the  Public Service Reform Plan 2014 to 2016 in the Education and Training sector.  A dedicated Programme Management Office (PMO) oversees the development and support the implementation of the Department's Integrated Reform Delivery Plan (IRDP).  The office has a full-time Programme Manager and a full-time Shared Services Adviser. The office reports to the Education and Training Reform Board which is chaired by the Secretary General and whose membership comprises members of the Management Advisory Committee (MAC). Appendix 1.

### Reform in Early Years settings and Schools

Currently priority school related projects within the IRDP are overseen directly by the Education and Training Reform Board.  In addition, a National Implementation Advisory Group, chaired by the Secretary General of the Department, oversees the implementation of the National Literacy and Numeracy Strategy.

### Reform in the Further Education and Training Sector (ETB/SOLAS)

A dedicated Project Office manages and co-ordinates reform relating to the Education and Training Boards and SOLAS.  A full time programme manager has been assigned at Principal Officer level. The office reports  to a high level ETB/SOLAS Programme Board which is chaired by the Secretary General and whose membership comprises  the  CEO  of  SOLAS,  CEOs  from  two  ETBs,  the  Secretary  General  Secretary  of  Education  and Training Boards Ireland, Assistant Secretaries and Principal Officers from across the Department and staff from the Programme Management Office.  In addition a Strategy Implementation Advisory Committee (SIAC) chaired by an Assistant Secretary from the Department, has been established by SOLAS to oversee the implementation of the Further Education and Training Strategy.

### Reform in the Higher Education and Training Sector

A Programme Board for higher education reform has been established and is chaired by the Deputy Secretary with  responsibility  for  this  area.    Membership  of  the  Higher  Education  Programme  Board  comprises representatives from the Irish Universities Association, Institutes of Technology Ireland and the Dublin Institute of  Technology  as  well  as  the  Assistant  Secretary  with  responsibility  for  Public  Service  Reform  and  Principal Officers from across the Department and staff from the programme management office.  In addition a Strategy Implementation Oversight Group (IOG) chaired by the Secretary General has been established to oversee the implementation of the Higher Education Strategy.

### Reform in the Department and its Agencies

Priority Projects within the IRDP 2015 relating to the Department and its agencies are overseen directly by the Education and Training Reform Board.  Individual projects are overseen by specific project steering groups.

## Section 2 Departmental / Sector Specific Reforms

The  Minister  for  Education  and  Skills  is  driving  the  implementation  of  an  ambitious  sector  wide  reform programme, underpinned by the most significant programme of legislative reform undertaken in the Education and Training sector for over a decade.    The Department is taking a 'whole of system' approach to this reform in the belief that this is the most effective way to deliver meaningful change and better outcomes for all learners. The programme centres on the four key goals of the Department's Strategy Statement 2015 to 2017:

### Learning for Life

We want an education and training system that provides all learners with the knowledge and skills they need to participate fully in society and the economy.

### Improving Quality and Accountability

We  want  to  provide  for  the  delivery  of  a  high  quality  education  and  training  experiences  for  everyone  and improve accountability for educational outcomes across the system.

### Supporting Inclusion and Diversity

We want an education and training system that welcomes and meaningfully includes learners with disabilities and  special  educational  needs,  learners  from  disadvantaged  communities/backgrounds  and  those  with language, cultural and social differences.

### Building the Right Systems and Infrastructure

We want a modern, flexible education and training system which makes the best use of available resources.

The Department is working closely with national agencies and other Government Departments to implement this ambitious programme of reform.

### 2.1 Departmental/Sector Specific Reform Projects:  Learning for Life

#### 2.1.1 Implementation of the Literacy and Numeracy Strategy to 2020

The  National  Literacy  and  Numeracy  Strategy  (2020)  drives  the  implementation  of  a  programme  of  reform focused  on  curriculum,  assessment  and  teaching  practices  to  underpin  a  high  quality,  relevant  learner experience and to enhance learning outcomes in early year's settings, primary and post-primary schools. Many of  the  targets  set  in  2014  for  the  Strategy  were  already  met  ahead  of  schedule  in  2014,  and  the  Minister announced  that  the  interim  review  of  the  Strategy,  originally  planned  to  take  place  in  2016  will  be  brought forward to 2015.

In  2015  the  focus  of  the  Literacy  and  Numeracy  Strategy  relating  to  Learning  for  Life  will  be  on  the  further implementation of Aistear , the curriculum framework for early years education; further reform of the primary and post  primary  curriculum;  the  continued  monitoring  of  standards  of  achievement  and  practice  in  schools  to ensure  implementation  of  the  strategy;  and  the  further  development  of  evaluation  in  schools.  The  Interim Review of the Strategy will enable new targets to be set, including targets for sub-groups of students (such as those in DEIS Band 1 schools) where greater improvements in literacy and numeracy standards are required. These targets will be used to measure outcomes resulting from strategy implementation to 2020.

#### 2.1.2 Junior Cycle Reform

The new Junior Cycle is designed to promote, amongst other things, creativity and innovation.  It will support learners in learning how to learn, and in taking responsibility for their own learning.

The first phase of Junior Cycle Reform commenced with the introduction of English in September 2014. The replacement of the existing junior certificate and in particular the new focus on assessment poses significant challenges  for  the  Department  and  the  sector.  This  requires  a  comprehensive  programme  of  continuing professional development for teachers and principals which is being led by a dedicated team (Junior Cycle for Teachers).  The  development  of  the  necessary  curriculum  documents  and  subject  specifications  are  the responsibility  of  the  NCCA.  Implementation  of  this  major  reform  is  subject  to  intense  consultations  with  the education partners.

#### 2.1.3 Learning for Life reforms in further education and training and in higher education

There are major reform programmes underway in both further education and training and in higher education. Driven by the Department's vision and the need to invest in people, these reforms will see the implementation of the further education and training strategy and the higher education strategy to improve performance and accountability and provide learners with the opportunities to reach their full potential and contribute to Ireland's economic, social and cultural development. Learning for Life initiatives for 2015 include the development of a new National Skills Strategy, further roll out of targeted Education and Training initiatives under the Skills to

#### 2.1.4 Development of a new National Skills Strategy

The  development  of  a  new  National  Skills  Strategy  will  provide  a  crucial  framework  for  understanding  and providing coherent responses to the evolving skills requirements of society and the economy at regional and national level and to ensure that individuals have the right skills to participate fully in society and reach their full potential.  This  will  encompass  all  levels  of  the  education  and  training  system,  with  a  particular  focus  on synergies  between  the  higher  and  further  education  sectors.   It  will  build  on  reforms  to  date,  including  the development of a strong  network  of  engaged  Higher  Education  Institutions  which  can  meet  Ireland's  human capital  needs  across  the  spectrum  of  skills  areas  and  a  co-ordinated  mechanism  to  engage  with  industry  to ensure alignment of FET with local, regional and national skills demand.

#### 2.1.5 Activation Agenda: Targeted Education and Training Initiatives

As part of the Action Plan for Jobs and Pathways to Work, the Department is continuing to develop high quality education  and  training  to  support  unemployed  people  to  return  to  employment  by  accessing  reskilling opportunities appropriate to their needs.    The Skills to Work brand, developed by Government, represents a coordinated approach to presenting the range of enterprise-focused targeted education and training programmes  available to jobseekers. Skills  to  Work includes ICT conversion courses, Springboard, Momentum,  Skillnets  Jobseeker  initiatives,  and  two  programmes  from  the  Department  of  Social  Protection, JobBridge and JobsPlus.

#### 2.1.6 Enhancement of employer engagement

A strategic goal for the Further Education and Training and Higher Education reform programmes is to ensure that  the  education  system  meets  the  spectrum  of  skills  needs  to  support  economic  development  and  job creation. Engagement with employers is key to ensuring that education and training provision is aligned with the needs of the economy and maximises the potential for learners to participate in the labour market.

In  line  with  the  actions  and  deliverables  in  the  FET  Implementation  Plan  and  Higher  Education  Performance Framework, structures to enhance engagement between providers, employers and other stakeholders ensure access to quality labour market intelligence are being examined to ensure the development of the most effective mechanisms for identifying and communicating skills needs at a regional and national level.

In addition as part of the implementation of the FET strategy an assessment of the level of training provision for those in employment will be undertaken to determine where gaps need to be addressed.

#### 2.1.7 Review of Apprenticeship Training

The report of an independent Review Group on apprenticeship training was published in January 2014.  The report contains a large number of recommendations for the extension of apprenticeship to new sectors and for modernising the existing apprenticeship system.  An apprenticeship Implementation Plan was published in June 2014.  New standards and curricula will be developed and rolled out for existing apprenticeships in 2015.  In addition,  a  call  for  proposals  from  employers  and  education  and  training  providers  for  the  development  of apprenticeship  programmes  in  new  areas  is  underway,  managed  by  the  new  Apprenticeship  Council.    With strong engagement from employers and providers, both the updated and new programmes will enhance the supply of job-ready personnel in key areas as well as providing a strong basis for successful and sustainable careers for young people.  Initial work on legislation on longer term governance in the apprenticeship area will also be undertaken in 2015.

#### 2.1.8 Improving transitions and removing barriers to progression between level of education and training

The Department is committed to ensuring learners are provided with more flexible learning options and that they are enabled to move through and across the continuum of education as it suits their education/training needs and requirements through life.  In addition a Transition Reform Steering Group has been established to address concerns that the dual use of the leaving Certificate to a) assess second level outcomes and b) to provide the basis  for  selection  and  entry  into  higher  education  institutions  is  having  negative  effects  on  teaching  and learning  in  post  primary  schools.      Reform  is  also  addressing  any  problematic  predictability  in  the  Leaving Certificate Examinations and work is ongoing to offer broader entry routes into third level through reducing the number of degree programmes in higher education.

### 2.2 Departmental/Sector Specific Reform Projects: Improving Quality and Accountability

#### 2.2.1 Early Years sector

The Department is working closely with the Department of Children and Youth Affairs on quality improvement within the early-years sector. It will support the operation of Better Start the newly established quality support service for the sector which will deploy a team of early year's specialists as mentors/advisers working with early-years services on quality improvement. During 2015, the NCCA will publish a web-based Aistear and Síolta Practice Guide which will host a range of interactive resources and materials in support of quality in practice. A new Early Years Education Advisory Group will be established.

To  complement  the  work  of  the  Child  and  Family  Agency  pre-school  inspectorate  (TUSLA),  the  DES Inspectorate  is  planning  for  the  introduction  of  focused  inspections  on  the  quality  of  educational  provision  in early childhood education settings participating in the Free Pre-School Year Programme.

#### 2.2.2 Increasing autonomy and accountability of schools

The Programme for Government includes commitments to devolve greater autonomy to schools.  A consultation exercise will precede the development of firm policy proposals to grant greater autonomy to schools, while ensuring that proper accountability mechanisms are in place, and this is currently under consideration in the Department.

#### 2.2.3 Implementation of new School self-evaluation (SSE) model and roll out of new models of school inspection in all schools

The Department continues to roll out the new self-evaluation model for all primary and post-primary schools. The new self-evaluation model articulates a clear set of standards for schools and focuses on outcomes for the learner, the objective of which is to enable school improvement. Work in 2015 will focus on supporting schools to  use  the  criteria  provided  in  the  SSE  Guidelines  to  determine  where  improvements  are  needed  and  to  set realistic  and  measurable targets to improve pupil/student learning. It will also focus on ensuring that schools provide  meaningful  short  summaries  of  their  SSE  reports  and  school  improvement  plans  to  their  school communities.  Preparation  of  criteria  to  assist  schools  to  engage  in  SSE  of  leadership  and  management,  in consultation with relevant partners will also be undertaken.

The roll out of a new range of inspection models for all schools will continue in 2015.  These new inspection models  increase  the  efficiency  and  effectiveness  of  inspection  and  they  support  the  targeting  of  resources where the need for assistance and support is greatest.  Work in 2015 will focus on continuing to implement a comprehensive  programme  of  evaluation  using  a  range  of  new  and  recently  reformed  inspection  models, revising supporting guidelines and regulations,  and developing and piloting new elements relating to curriculum evaluation, student wellbeing and the inspection of special education provision.

#### 2.2.4 Gaeltacht Education Review

As part of its work to support the implementation of the 20-Year Strategy for Irish, the Department commenced a major review of education provision  in the Gaeltacht in 2014 and this  work will be advanced in 2015. It is planned that the current research phase of the work will conclude and that policy options to improve education provision in Gaeltacht areas will be developed.

#### 2.2.5 Implement the FET Strategy and new governance arrangements for the Further Education and Training Sector including SOLAS

SOLAS was established in 2013 to oversee the operation of the Further Education and Training sector.  A five year  Further  Education  and  Training  Strategy  and  related  implementation  plan  was  published  in  2014  which identifies key objectives and targets for the sector.  As the new oversight and funding agency SOLAS will play a key role in relation to quality and accountability within the Further Education and Training Sector.  A new funding model based on performance levels will be implemented through the agreement of annual Further Education and training Service Plans with the ETBs.  The first Service Plan was published for 2014 and the model is being further developed and improved for 2015 and subsequent years.

#### 2.2.6 Introducing a Systems Performance Framework for Higher Education provision

Implementation of the Higher Education Strategy is ongoing and involves the development of a new system of governance  and  a  new  performance  framework  to  increase  accountability  of  autonomous  higher  education institutions for public funding and performance against national priorities.

A National Framework for PhD Education has been developed by the HEA/QQI in consultation with a number of stakeholders. It commits the stakeholders to the highest standards in the provision of doctoral education and research so that students, employers, etc may have full confidence in the quality of Irish doctoral education.

Work in this area comes under the governance of a Strategy Implementation Oversight Group (IOG) chaired by the Secretary General.

#### 2.2.7 Enhancing teaching in Education

To  ensure  that  early  year's  practitioners  are  equipped  with  the  skills  and  competences  to  work  in  the  early year's  sector,  DES  will  be  carrying  out  a  a  review  of  education  and  training  programmes  that  lead  to qualifications  in  early  years  care  and  education.  The  consultation  process  for  the  review  will  be  launched  in January 2015.

As part of the ongoing progression towards a fully regulated teaching body within the framework of the Teaching Council Acts changes have been made to the content and pedagogical approaches included in Initial Teacher Education (ITE) programmes to prepare teachers better for the classroom and a number of ITE programmes for the Further Education and Training sector have been accredited.  Plans are in place to commence Part 5 of the Teaching Council Act (Fitness to Teach) following amendments to the Act to strengthen provision in this area and to provide for Garda Vetting arrangements.  The Council will continue to progress the quality agenda for teaching in 2015 through further work on the review of entry criteria for initial teacher education programmes, the development of induction and probation procedures and progression towards a framework for CPD.

Work by the Department on the digital strategy for schools (see section 3.2 Digital Government/ICT) is aimed at maximizing the use of digital technologies to transform teaching, learning and assessment at primary and post primary level.

#### 2.2.9 International Education

A  review  of  Investing  in  Global  Relationships:  Ireland's  International  Education  Strategy  2010  -  2015  will  be undertaken in 2015 to support the development of a new Government action plan for the promotion of Ireland as a center for international education.

The International Education Mark (IEM) was proposed in Ireland's International Education Strategy 2010 - 2015 as a means of enhancing the quality and promotion of international education in Ireland.  QQI is responsible for authorising  use  of the  IEM  by  providers  of  programmes  of  education  and  training.   The  IEM confirms  that  a provider has demonstrated their capacity and commitment to provide a high-quality educational experience to international students.

The  final  Code  of  Practice  Policy  for  Providers  of  Programmes  of  Education  and  Training  to  International Students will be proposed for adoption by the Board of QQI in Q1 2015.  QQI will begin to accept applications for  authorisation  to  use  the  IEM  from  English  Language  Training  Organisations  in  mid-2015  and  higher education providers will then be able to apply in the second half of the year.

### 2.3 Departmental/Sector Specific Reform Projects: Supporting Inclusion and Diversity

#### 2.3.1 Reform of school practice

2.3.1.1  Enrolment: Consultation is underway to ensure that the process of enrolling children in schools is more open,  equitable  and  consistent.    Legislative  proposals  have  been  published  and  a  Draft  Bill  has  been considered by the Oireachtas committee. Subject to Government decisions, it is expected that the Education (Admission  to  School)  Bill  will  proceed  to  publication  and  enactment  in  2015.    Prior  to  commencing  the legislation, the Department will consider whether it is necessary to give a lead-in period for schools to update their enrolment policies and practices so as to facilitate a smooth transition to the statutory requirements.

2.3.1.2  Parent  and  Student  Engagement: The  Minister  has  committed  to  publishing  the  arrangements  for establishing a Parent and Student Charter. The purpose of the Charter is to strengthen the position of parents within the school system and to bring about a culture change in how schools engage with and involve parents (and students in an age related manner).

#### 2.3.2 Increasing choice of primary school type

The Department is committed to providing for greater diversity of school type and to implementing a planning approach that has a greater emphasis on parental preferences. During 2015 the Department will continue to support the process of divesting of Catholic primary schools in 28 areas.  Work in 2015 will focus on advancing identified options for alternative patronage provision.

#### 2.3.3 Promoting inclusiveness in schools

Following the publication in July 2014 of Forum on Patronage and Pluralism in the Primary Sector: Progress to Date and Future Directions, work will continue in association with primary school patron bodies to disseminate good  practice  and  to  encourage  greater  inclusivity  in  schools.  Work  will  continue  on  the  development  of  a curriculum in Education about Religion and Beliefs and Ethics in 2015.

New inspection models will evaluate and report on the quality of education provision for marginalised leaners and learners with special education needs during 2015.

#### 2.3.4 New allocation model for special needs teaching resources

A new model for the allocation of resource and learning support teachers for all mainstream primary and post primary  schools  will  be  developed  and  proposals  for  its  implementation  will  be  submitted  to  Government  for decision.

#### 2.3.5 Widening participation in higher education

A new National Access Plan for higher education will be developed to promote access for disadvantaged groups and to put in place coherent pathways from second level education, from further education and other non-traditional entry routes with the objective of progressing towards the Bologna Process objective of ensuring that the student body entering, participating in and completing higher education at all levels reflects the diversity of Ireland's population.

#### 2.4.5 Further Education and Training Data

There is a critical need to improve the availability of detailed information on the involvement and experience of learners  accessing  further  education  and  training  interventions  and  on  the  courses  provided  through  further education and training interventions. #### 2.4.6 Structural Reform in Higher Education

Implementation of key aspects of the higher education landscape reform process will continue to roll out during 2015. It  provides  the  legal  underpinning  for  the  mergers  of  Institutes  of Technology, the establishment of Technological Universities and the reform of governing bodies in the Institutes of Technology.  Work will also continue through the HEA to further develop the mechanisms and structures for regional planning and to enable clustering of HEIs.

An Expert Group has been established to make recommendations on the future funding of higher education and this group is due to report by the end of the year.

#### 2.4.7 Centralisation  of  student  grants  function  and  further  roll  out  of  new  online  student  grant application system, SUSI

The new single on-line student grant application system will, following the current transition period, reduce the number of grant awarding bodies from sixty-six to one. It  also reforms  the payment system for students, introducing monthly electronic payments directly to students' bank accounts. The system is progressively taking on all new grant applications and annual grant renewals.

## Section 4 Haddington Road Agreement Reforms

### 4.1 Additional Working Hours

Revised arrangements for the use of additional working hours under the Haddington Road Agreement for the Education  and  Training  Sector  have  been  put  in  place.    The  gross  savings  from  implementation  of  the Haddington Road Agreement in the Education and Training sector were agreed with D/PER and have been built into the Education VOTE figure for 2014 and 2015.

#### 4.1.1 Utilisation of additional hours in teaching (60,500 teachers)

The most significant measure was on the teaching side where the additional hours are utilised entirely to amend the existing supervision and substitution scheme.  Additional hours are required from teachers under the supervision and substitution scheme and in addition to this payments under the scheme were discontinued with effect from the commencement of the 2013/14 school year giving an annual saving of c. €116M per annum for the lifetime of the Agreement.

#### 4.1.2 Review in relation to additional hours for Special Needs Assistants (10,965 SNAs)

Under  the  terms  of  the Public  Service  Agreement  2010-2014 ,  discussions  took  place  that  led  to agreement  on  changed  attendance  arrangements  for  Special  Needs  Assistants  (SNAs)  that  give schools  the  option  of  an  additional  two  hours  working  time  per  week  from  each  SNA.  These  new attendance arrangements are the subject of a LRC review in order to assess if the additional hours are being utilised in the most effective and appropriate manner and in order to achieve agreement on any changes deemed necessary in terms of the arrangement agreed for teachers.  This review is ongoing under the auspices of the LRC.

#### 4.1.3 Use of additional hours by third level lecturers (9,000) and non-academic staff (8,500)

Increased productivity from the additional time being delivered by the staff in third level institutions is being utilised to manage  workloads  and  backlogs  and  maintain service delivery.    Measures implemented include elimination  of  time  off  for  church  holidays,  reduced  extra  weighting  for  evening lecturing,  working  an  additional  78  hours  per  annum,  reduction  of  fees  for  examination  marking payments  etc.    On  the  non-academic  staff  side  a  reduction  in  the  reliance  on  overtime  will  provide savings of €1.2m per annum.  This is set in the context of increasing volumes of business and falling staff numbers for more than five years.

Implementation  of  these  measures  in  the  Education  sector  is  complete.  Reduction  in  the  number  of personnel required in third level Institutions to reflect the additional time now being delivered by all staff in the sector will yield a further €5m in both 2015 and 2016.  This measure may necessitate a voluntary redundancy (VR) package or other such measure if staff numbers do not reduce naturally over time. However, at the moment the requisite savings associated with these measures are being delivered and have been built into the Education VOTE figure for 2014 and 2015.

#### 4.1.4 Other staff (5,500), including Civil Service (1,500)

Other staff (administrative, management, support, research and others) are required to work additional time.   Because  the  overwhelming  majority  of  posts  in  question  are  subject  to  a  strict  moratorium  on replacement,  the  scope  for  making  savings  on  recruitments  and  appointments  is  negligible.   For  the most part, employers in the sector are using this additional time to manage workloads and backlogs and maintain service delivery in a context in which they have had increasing volumes of business and falling staff numbers for more than five years.  No directly quantifiable savings can be attributed specifically to this  additional  time  as  the  benefits  will  find  expression  in  increased  service  enhancements  and productivity rather than in direct cost savings.

DES is using the additional time to manage workloads, backlogs, maintain service delivery due to ECF restrictions and pay and conditions changes from Haddington Road Agreement, reduction in overtime costs (20%) and to undertake a review of Business Process Improvement projects in various areas of the Department.

#### 4.2.1 Proposal for grade rationalisation
