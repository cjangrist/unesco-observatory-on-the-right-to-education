## Questionnaire

### Laws and policies (preamble and paras 4 and 7 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### A1a. Please  describe any  specific national or sub-national laws, legislation or legal framework(s) in your country that govern the promotion of global citizenship education (GCED) and/or education for sustainable development (ESD). Explain whether GCED and/or ESD themes 1  are mentioned explicitly or are inferred and what types or levels of education are covered.

The preamble of the Act of 14 December 2016 Education Law stipulates that education in the Republic of Poland is a common good of the whole society, it is guided by the principles contained in the Constitution of the Republic of Poland, as well as the indications laid out in the Universal Declaration  of  Human  Rights,  International  Covenant  on  Civil  and  Political  Rights,  and  the Convention on the Rights of the Child. The education system in Poland shall provide inter alias:

-shaping pro-social attitudes with pupils, including through the possibility of participation in volunteering activities, conducive to active participation in social life;

-disseminating knowledge about the principles of sustainable development among children and youth, and shaping attitudes fostering its implementation on a local, national and global scale;

-dissemination of knowledge among children and youth on the principles of rational nutrition and prevention of food waste;

-shaping entrepreneurship attitudes in pupils conducive to active participation in economic life, including through the application of innovative programme, organisational, or methodological solutions in the education process (article 1 of the Act of 14 December Education Law ).

##### Educational (upbringing) function of the school

Schools and institutions of the Polish system of education respect human rights and support the educational role of the family. The educational activity of the school is one of the basic goals of the  state  educational  policy.  The  system  of  values  presented  at  school  should  provide  each student with the conditions necessary for his/her development, prepare him/her to fulfil family and civic  obligations  on  the  basis  of  the  principles  of  solidarity,  democracy,  tolerance,  justice  and freedom. During coherent activities combining education with upbringing, the sense of individual, cultural, national, regional and ethnic identity is strengthened and the sense of dignity of oneself and others is formed.

The aim of upbringing is to support the pupils in comprehensive development aimed at achieving full physical, emotional, intellectual, spiritual and social maturity. Introducing students to the world of  values,  including  dedication,  cooperation,  solidarity,  altruism,  preparation  for  building  social relationships  conducive  to  the  safe  development  of  the  student  takes  place  not  only  during compulsory educational activities, but also during school trips, extracurricular and out of school activities.

In 2019, the regulations also introduced the obligation to conduct an annual diagnosis of students' developmental  needs  in  the  school  environment,  including  protective  and  risk  factors,  with particular emphasis on the risks associated with the use of psychotropic substances, substitutes and new psychoactive substances. The head of the school or facility or an employee of the school or facility authorized by the head is responsible for the diagnosis.

The educational and preventive program is adopted by the parents' council in agreement with the pedagogical  council.  Therefore,  it  is  the  parents  who  have  a  significant  impact  on  programs, projects,  undertakings,  and  initiatives,  including  those  implemented  in  cooperation  with  nongovernmental organizations, in accordance with the earlier definition of the areas of intervention.

The  educational  and  preventive  program  should  include  educational  and  preventive  activities according to the knowledge and developmental age of students, the standards of respect for the dignity of others, responsibility for their own decisions, and the student's rights.

Preventive actions also relate to the methods of counteracting the manifestations of intolerance, including for national and ethnic minorities. The aims of education and teaching content adjusted to the age, experiences and cognitive abilities of students are defined in the core curriculum. The main duty of the school is to shape patriotic and civic attitudes, as well as a sense of national, cultural  and  individual  identity  and  awareness.  Hence,  it  becomes  necessary  to  build  good relationships between parents, teachers and students. A uniform approach to education which takes  into  account  universal  values  is  the  right  direction  of  eliminating  or  minimizing  difficult educational situations and inappropriate behaviour of children and adolescents.

The school set of curricula and the educational and preventive curriculum of the school form a coherent  whole  and  must  take  into  account  all  the  requirements  described  in  the  new  core curriculum. Their preparation and implementation are the task of both the entire school and each teacher.

The purpose of the above-mentioned interactions is to equip students with the knowledge that all people have equal rights, regardless of where they are born, what they look like, what religion they profess and what their material status is. Schools are obliged to respect human rights, and in educational activities such as: history, ethics and knowledge of society, the content of teaching directly relates to shaping the attitudes of tolerance, issues of human rights and their protection, and civic attitudes.

The strategic framework for civic education and education for sustainable development is included in the main programming document on skills in Poland. It is the Integrated Skills Strategy 2030 (general part) adopted by the Council of Ministers on January 25, 2019. The Integrated Skills Strategy  2030  is  consistent  with  the  main  national  strategy  -  Strategy  for  Responsible Development adopted by the Council of Ministers on February 14, 2017.

The Integrated Skills Strategy 2030 also refers to the government programmes supporting the development of civil society, such as:

-Civil Society Organisations Development Programme for 2018-2030;

-Solidarity  Corps  -  The  Programme  for  the  Support  and  Development  of  Long-term Volunteerism for 2018-2030;

-Government  programme  of  Support  for  the  Development  of  Polish  Scout  and  Scouting Organisations for 2018-2030.

The Integrated Skills Strategy 2030 (detailed part). Policy to develop skills in line with the idea of lifelong learning , adopted  by the Council of Ministers on 28 December  2020,  is the operationalization of the priorities and main directions of activities included in the Integrated Skills Strategy 2030 (general part).

##### Children and youth participation in sociall life

The Council of Children and Youth of the Republic of Poland to the Minister of Education and Science ( Rada Dzieci i Młodzieży Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej przy Ministrze Edukacji i Nauki Narodowej  -  earlier Minister of National Education) has  been  operating  since  2016  https://www.gov.pl/web/edukacja-i-nauka/rada-dzieci-i-mlodziezy-rzeczypospolitej-polskiej-przyministrze-edukacji-i-nauki.  The  Council's  tasks  include  expressing  opinions  and  submitting proposals on issues concerning children and adolescents in the scope of matters covered by the department of government administration for education and upbringing, in particular presenting opinions on planned changes and proposals of solutions, as well as promoting patriotic and civic attitudes and activity of young people in public life, who play a key role in creating civil society. The Council consists of 16 members and their deputies appointed by the Minister of Education and Science, one member and one deputy from each province.

### C. Teacher education (including the training of trainers and educators) (paras 4 and 33-37 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### C1a. Please describe in which ways GCED and ESD are mainstreamed in initial training and in  continuing  professional  development  of  teachers,  trainers  and  educators.  Indicate whether all teachers, trainers and educators or only some are required to receive training to teach GCED and/or ESD.

The content of GCED and ESD is present in the scope specified in the provisions of the Regulation of the Minister of Science and Higher Education of 25 July 2019 on the standard of education preparing for the teaching profession (Dziennik Ustaw [Polish Journal of Laws] of 2019, item 1450, as amended). The regulation is the basis for the creation of teacher education programs for fulltime and postgraduate studies by universities.

The  obligatory  tasks  of  public  in-service  training  institutions  run  by  local  government  (the voivodeship, poviat or commune) include organizing and providing in-service training for teachers, inter alias, on the implementation of the core curricula, including the development of curricula. Therefore, in-service teacher training institutions conduct teacher training to implement all the content contained in the core curricula defined for individual subjects and types of classes at individual educational stages.

Teachers  receive  proposals  of  training  /  improvement  in  the  field  of  global  education  and sustainable development, including:

-in the Education Development Centre (ORE  a nationwide education institution, subordinated to the Ministry of National Education),

-in local vocational training institutions,

-through the activities of the Global Education Coordinators network (run by ORE),

-as part of the "Etyka nie tylko dla Smyka' [ Ethics not only for Kid ] programmme.

The  proposed  forms  of  professional  development  take  into  account  the  issues  (in  terms  of knowledge, values, attitudes and behaviour) concerning:

-cultural diversity / tolerance,

-human rights,

-climate change,

-non-violence,

-sustainable consumption and production.

Elements of global and civic education at primary and secondary school levels are introduced: cross-curricularly (topics are included in different subjects, but not in all of them), through the implementation of integrated content (mainly at the level of early childhood education).

In  Poland,  there  is  also  the Civic  Education  Centre ( CEO -  https://ceo.org.pl/programy)  -  it implements educational programs (annual or several years) that involve teaching staff, principals and  students,  conducts  trainings,  workshops  for  pedagogical  councils,  Internet  courses  and postgraduate studies for teachers and principals, publishes textbooks on knowledge about society and educational materials for work with young people.

Currently, the CEO implements approx. 37 educational programmes, including:

-programs that introduce activating teaching methods, formative assessment and develop teacher collaboration, changing the school's work culture;

-programs encouraging students to undertake activities in the field of: voluntary work, selfgovernance and entrepreneurship at school and in the local environment;

-global and ecological education programs explaining how the modern world works, how to take care of it and of the environment and on each other responsibly;

##### General comment by the Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights , concerning the issues of human rights and civic education

In its information, the Ministry of National Education cites a number of documents and programs that formally and significantly define the framework of the education system, its basic goals, tasks and values. Human rights issues or elements of civic education appear in the core curricula. However, there is a lack of good educational materials and interesting lesson scenarios. The rights are discussed in the context of fulfilling obligations.

The term of "gender equality" does not exist in the core curriculum. The term "tolerance" appears in the context of shaping civic attitudes, among others responsibility, concern for the common good and tolerance. It is not known whether the scope of understanding of this term is sufficient. The  core  curriculum  provides  for  school  classes  on  non-discrimination.  In  practice,  it  looks different.

The participation of young people in the decision-making process in matters of school life seems to be a good indicator of the condition of civic education in Polish schools. In the alternative report 6 to the government report on the implementation of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, it is noted  that  formal  education  law  gives  schools  and  students  the  tools  to  take  into  account children's  voices  and  integrate  them  into  school  life.  The  student  council  is  an  institution  well enacted  in  law,  however  it  still  remains  a  missed  opportunity,  and  sometimes  even  a  fiction. Meanwhile, research by UNICEF Poland 7   shows that the vast majority of children wish to have greater influence on decisions taken at school.

School  statutes  that  rank  as  school  constitutions  often  contain  long  catalogues  of  rights  and duties. In general, however, these are provisions about various privileges and rights resulting from the school's tasks, sometimes mixed without hierarchy with human rights. A catalogue of rights constructed in this way is an example of a misunderstanding of the essence of human rights.

Generally, students know the names of international documents, the dates of their creation, can name a few laws, but do not really understand what these rights mean in everyday life. For example, that human rights result from the relationship between power authority and the individual, and the authority has a duty to respect their rights, that they cannot limit them without  appropriate  statutory  provisions,  and  these  must  comply  with  standards,  etc.  This conclusion seems to be confirmed by the opinions of academic teachers.

Recommendations  included  in  the  alternative  report  show  significant differences in the assessment of the state  of  education  on  human  rights  and  civic  education  by  the  Ministry  of National Education and by non-governmental organizations 11 :

'Conducting systematic child's rights education among children as well as professionals working with children and for their benefit.'

'Expanding teaching content regarding civic education by information showing children and youth mechanisms of involvement in social life, motivating and preparing them for full-value engagement in decision-making.'

'Proactive conduct by the government of activities to combat all manifestations of discrimination.' 'Implementing systemic anti-discrimination education.'
