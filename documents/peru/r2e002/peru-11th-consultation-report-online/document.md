##### Please list the prohibited grounds of discrimination in education as laid out in legal texts:

Law No. 30512, Law on Institutes and Schools of Higher Education and the public career of their teachers, establishes in its article 7 the principles of Higher Education, among which are the principles of:

-	'Social inclusion' (literal d), which allows all people, without discrimination, to exercise their rights, take advantage of their abilities, enhance their identities and take advantage of the opportunities that their environment offers them, accessing quality public services, so that cultural, economic, social, ethnic and geographical factors become facilitators for access to higher education.

-	'Equity' (literal f), which seeks to ensure that the educational service reaches all people, avoiding situations of discrimination and inequality due to origin, race, sex, language, religion, opinion, economic condition or any other nature. Likewise, it promotes policies of positive recognition of cultural diversity, to this end they guarantee reasonable adjustments that allow the access and permanence of vulnerable or disabled populations.

-	'Interculturality' (literal h), which indicates that the cultural, ethnic and linguistic diversity of the country is assumed to be rich, and finds sustenance in the recognition and respect for differences, as well as in mutual knowledge and learning attitude. for harmonious coexistence and exchange between the various cultures of the world.

Along these lines, the Regulation of Law No. 30512, in its article 17, states that training in IES and EEST is developed taking into account the approaches of equal opportunities, Human Rights, Environment, Disability,  ender and Interculturality.

Likewise, the ' eneral Academic  uidelines for Higher Education Institutes and Technological Higher Education Schools' and the ' eneral Academic  uidelines for Technical-Productive Education Centers', in the description of their pedagogical approach, emphasize that Educational institutions must respond to the purposes, educational policies and transversal approaches, such as the rights approach, inclusive approach or attention to diversity, intercultural approach, gender equality approach, environmental approach, intergenerational approach, disability approach and differential, among others that you consider relevant.

On the other hand, Legislative Decree No. 1375, incorporates article 41 to Law No. 28044, which establishes as one of the objectives of the ETP, the attention to vulnerable populations with educational services that promote their qualification for the work of dependent or independent way.

Likewise, it incorporates article 41-A, which describes the characteristics of the ETP, among which are:

-	Flexible (literal b), because it responds to the heterogeneity of the students and the peculiarity of their contexts.

-	Inclusive (literal d), because it allows all people without any discrimination to exercise their rights, take advantage of their abilities and potential, reinforce their autonomy and develop under equal conditions, respecting and valuing different cultural visions, conceptions of well-being and development of the various ethnic-cultural groups, promoting access to an education that guarantees the right to cultural identity.

Law N° 30220, Article 5 of this Law (University Law) establishes the principles governing universities, among which are the following: 5.7 Meritocracy; 5.8 Pluralism, tolerance, intercultural dialogue and inclusion; 5.10 Affirmation of human life and dignity; 5.14 The best interest of the student; 5.15 Relevance of teaching and research to social reality; 5.16 Rejection of all forms of violence, intolerance and discrimination.

In addition, according to Article 6 of the aforementioned Law, the university has the following purposes, among others: 6.4 To collaborate effectively in the affirmation of democracy, the rule of law and social inclusion; 6.7 To affirm and transmit the diverse cultural identities of the country; 6.10 To form free people in a free society.

Along these lines, the National Policy for Higher and Technical-Productive Education (PNESTP, because Spanish acronym) establishes nine (9) cross-cutting approaches that must be considered in the provision of educational services in order to meet the prioritized objectives and their respective guidelines. Thus, there are the following:

- Inclusive education approach: It is an analysis tool that allows taking into account, at institutional and systemic level, the diversity of needs, potentials and aspirations of young people and adults, before and throughout the educational process. Inclusive education has four elements. First, it is an ongoing, long-term process that seeks permanent feedback between diversity and learning processes.  Second, it seeks to identify and remove barriers of all kinds that impede the participation of young people and adults in education. Third, it seeks participation and completion of the educational process. And, fourth, it involves a particular emphasis on those groups of young people and adults who are at risk of vulnerability and/or exclusion.

- ender approach: This is an analytical tool that makes it possible, based on an evaluation of reality and social relations, to identify the roles, spaces and social attributes assigned to women and men, as well as the unequal power relations and asymmetries that are reproduced within society. In this way, it makes possible to identify and explain the causes that end up reproducing asymmetries and inequalities, constituting an essential input for the formulation, implementation and evaluation of measures that contribute to overcoming gender inequality, modifying asymmetrical relations between women and men, and

- Intercultural approach: This is an analytical tool based on the recognition of cultural differences as one of the pillars for the construction of a democratic society, based on the establishment of relations of equity and equality of opportunities and rights. The National Policy for Higher and Technical-Productive Education, approved by Supreme Decree No. 012-2020-MINEDU, (hereinafter PNESTP), raises as a public problem 'Population with inadequate skills to practice their profession and develop research and innovation', which It has been built through a systemic approach that considers access among its central elements.

In this regard, access addresses the problem related to the transition to Higher and Technical-Productive Education (ESTP because Spanish acronym). In the previous stage, aspects such as vocational guidance and the provision of information for decision-making are addressed. In the admission stage, aspects related to the identification of competencies or learning for access and the provision of student support mechanisms and scholarships and credits are addressed. This element also addresses the problem related to the passability of the population in general.

Likewise, the PNESTP includes, among other approaches, the Inclusive Education Approach, which is an analysis tool that allows duly taking into account, at an institutional and systemic level, the diversity of needs, potential and aspirations of young people and adults, before and throughout the training process. Inclusive education has four elements. Firstly, it is a constant and longterm process, which seeks permanent feedback between diversity and learning processes. Secondly, it seeks to identify and remove barriers of all kinds that prevent the participation of young people and adults in education. Third, it seeks participation and conclusion of the educational process. And, fourthly, it involves a particular emphasis on those groups of young people and adults who are at risk of vulnerability and/or exclusion.

Under this context, the PNESTP has 6 Priority Objectives (OP), OP1 being 'Increase the population's equitable access to Higher and Technical-Productive Education', which aims to provide various routes for equitable access, according to the potential and aspirations of basic education students and the population over 14 years of age. The mechanisms include the national evaluation of learning and strategies for vocational and labor orientation of basic education students and the population in general, for the continuity of their educational trajectories in the ESTP. It should be noted that this objective seeks to identify and reduce the barriers that limit access through support mechanisms for the population at greatest risk of exclusion.

It should be noted that the study programs of Technical - Productive and Higher Technological Education, whose training is applied and of a technical nature, must respond to a set of competencies that are required in the labor market, for this, the study plans that implement them, they must contemplate technical or specific competencies and competencies for employability, incorporating the learning contents necessary for their development, which ensure the comprehensive training of students with social and employment values, which allow them to adapt and insert themselves into a specific work space, to perform a specific function.

Without prejudice to this, in accordance with the ' eneral Academic  uidelines for Higher Education Institutes and Technological Higher Education Schools' and the ' eneral Academic  uidelines for Technical-Productive Education Centers', the public IES, EEST and CETPRO They must respond to the purposes, educational policies and transversal approaches, such as: the rights approach, inclusive approach or attention to diversity, intercultural approach, gender equality approach, environmental approach, intergenerational approach, disability and differential approach, among others that you consider relevant; which are indicated in the description of its pedagogical approach. These approaches guide the various educational processes at all times, imbuing themselves with the competencies that students are expected to develop.

Under this context, the IES, EEST and CETPRO take these approaches into consideration in a transversal manner, for the appropriate treatment of each student and in the own development of learning activities. The implementation of these approaches seeks to promote equitable treatment, free of violence and discrimination, respecting the right of all people to be educated with equal opportunities. To achieve this, some strategies are developed such as:

-	Develop methodological strategies in teaching/learning sessions to promote the equitable or proportional participation of women and men in all activities aimed at achieving the capabilities associated with the competency units of the study program.

-	Use of methodologies for the development of necessary attitudes that complement the comprehensive training of students, both on a personal and professional level.

-	The training strategy must incorporate flexibility criteria in training that facilitate methodological, curricular, resource-level adaptation, among others, that favor learning processes while respecting the pace and characteristics of each student.

In University Education

Regarding recognizing, validating and certifying non-formal and informal learning

The  eneral Academic  uidelines for the CETPRO, approved with RVM No. 188-2020-MINEDU, establishes the validation process through which a CETPRO recognizes the skills, abilities and performances acquired by a person in the educational or work field. Validation recognizes one or more teaching units or training modules and allows the continuity of studies with respect to a specific study plan. Validation can be carried out under different modalities: Recognition of prior learning, Validation between study plans and Validation by units of competence.

Recognition of prior learning is carried out when a CETPRO recognizes learning previously achieved within or outside the institutions of the Educational System, as long as these are linked to the competency units of a study program.

Validation between study plans is carried out when the following conditions are present: 1. Change of study plan, it is for students who began their studies with a study plan that has lost validity and must continue with a new plan, in which same or another authorized or licensed CETPRO. 2. Change of study program, when a student transfers to another study program at CETPRO or another formally authorized or licensed one.

Validation by units of competence is carried out when the following conditions are present: Certification of labor competencies and Modular Certification.

-	The Certification of labor competencies: The unit of labor competency described in the certificate of labor competency is validated, with the unit of competency associated with a study program. At the time of validation, the labor competency certificate must be valid and must be issued by an authorized certification center.

-	Modular certification: The unit of competency or units of competencies, if applicable, described in the modular certificate, are validated with the unit of competency associated with a study program. At the time of validation, the study program associated with the modular certificate, as well as the unit of competence to be validated, must be authorized or licensed.

Validation with Basic Education , in this modality, the performances acquired by a student throughout their training are validated. To do this, the student must have their modular certificate issued by the educational institution.

Regarding offering continuous training (such as training programs to acquire knowledge, skills, abilities or competencies for personal and professional development)

Technical-Productive Education (ETP), in the  eneral Academic  uidelines for the CETPRO, approved with RVM No. 188-2020-MINEDU, contemplates that the CETPRO can develop continuous training programs (PFC), through which a comprehensive and permanent educational process that develops throughout life. The PFC's objective is to acquire, perfect or update knowledge, skills, personal and/or professional abilities, to improve work performance. The PFC are provided in face-toface, blended or distance modalities and do not require authorization from the Local Management Units (U EL). The Optimization of the educational offer constitutes an improvement process that seeks to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of the provision of the public technological higher education service. The Optimization process seeks to improve the quality, sustainability and relevance in 343 public IESTs nationwide, impacting the technological higher education of 120 thousand students of these institutions.

As of June 2024, 26 regions have an Optimization plan approved by the Minedu. In this document, the regions have planned the actions and programmed the resources to close gaps in basic quality conditions with a view to licensing, according to the established schedule.

Through the DI ESUTPA Optimization and Strengthening team, technical assistance is provided to public IESTs to close gaps in the basic quality conditions (CBC) with a view to institutional licensing, with the aim that the offer is sustainable, relevant and consistent with regional social, productive and economic development. The Strategy for Strengthening the public offering prioritizes those institutes according to the licensing schedule, that is, those that will be presented soon.
