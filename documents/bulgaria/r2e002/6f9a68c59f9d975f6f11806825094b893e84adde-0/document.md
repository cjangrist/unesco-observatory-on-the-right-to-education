## INTRODUCTION

The strategic goal of the European Union until 2010 is the creation of the most competitive in the world, dynamic, based on knowledge economy, with more and better work places and higher social cohesion. The achievement of this goal requires exceptional attention to be paid to provide appropriate education and vocational training for improving the suitability of the labour force for employment. The public administration of Republic of Bulgaria, a country for which accession to the European Community is imminent, takes into consideration the European strategies, policies and  practices  in  the  field  of  the  continuing  vocational  training.  We  consider  as  leading  in  this respect  the  Lisbon  Strategy  till  2010,  the  life-long  learning  concept,  the  'Education  &  Training 2010' Programme, the European Employment Strategy and others.

During  the  period  of  transition  to  a  market  economy  and  the  accompanying  intensive process of restructuring the production, it's obvious a definite trend of discrepancy between the quality of the labour force and the new requirements. It is more strongly expressed in comparison with  the  future  requirements  of  the  national  labour  force,  having  in  mind  the  pre-accession processes and the developing globalization. There is a important task for the reform in education, and particularly in the vocational education and training in Bulgaria - to provide such conditions for vocational training, which will allow continuously keeping of the quality of the labour force and its suitability for employment in compliance with the requirements of the labour market and the  knowledge-based  economy.  This  task  can  be  fulfilled  by  continuous  vocational  training  for everyone on equal access and equal conditions. It is necessary a clear, concrete, target-defined in the  time  and  social  space  strategy,  secured  by  resources,  which  will  develop  the  continuing vocational training in the country. The Strategy is mutually bound with similar documents for the social and economic development: the Governmental Programme, the National Plan for Economic Development until 2006, the National Plan for Development until 2013 - in preparation now, the Employment Strategy for 2004-2010 and others.

The Purpose of  the  Strategy  is  to  help  the  development  of  the  reform  in  the  vocational education and training by creation of conceptual, methodological and planning conditions for its implementation.

Subject of the Strategy is the training of persons aged above 16 for earning, developing and perfecting vocational qualification in order to improve the suitability for employment, to assist the professional career and individual development.

The Goal of  the  Strategy  is  to  outline  the  national  priorities  for  development  of  the continuing vocational education in the context of the life-long learning as well as to point out the institutions responsible for the implementation.

## І.    PRESENT  SITUATION,  TRENDS  AND  CHALLENGES  IN  THE  FIELD  OF CONTINUING VOCATIONAL TRAINING

### 1. Present Situation and Trends in the Labour Market

The  labour  market  is  characterized  by  quantitative  and  qualitative  disbalance  during  the transition period. The demand of jobs considerably exceeds the supply of such. In the same time, the qualitative characteristics of the unemployed labour force do not correspond to the qualitative structure of the offered work places. The negative demographic situation deepens the discrepancies and  the  unwilling  effects  in  demanding  and  supplying  of  work  places.  The  negative  growth  in population and the considerable outside migration have an adverse effect on the absolute number and  the  structure  of  the  labour  resources  in  Bulgaria.  A  permanent  trend  for  decreasing  of  the active population is observed.

Predominant part of the total labour force are people graduated secondary schools - 55,7%; ones graduated high schools are 23,4%, and those with lower stages of education are 20,9%.

As  a  result  of  the  economic  stabilization  during  the  past  years,  the  unemployment  goes down. The unemployment level in the country for 2003 was 13,7%¹. High levels of the long-term unemployment and low educational and skill levels of the unemployed are still typical. Over 40 % of the long-term unemployed in 2003 were without any skills or had the lowest skill level. For the remaining part, as well as for many of the employed, a serious problem is de-qualification - this applies especially for the older-age groups. Considerable is the level of the youths' unemployment (28,1%). For many of the youths the reasons are the low level of education, the lack of a profession or lack of practical experience.

The qualitative structure of the labour force in the regions is different.

The distribution of the unemployment in the regions and towns is uneven; the same applies for the qualitative structure of the unemployed.

The demand for labour force² changes depending on the general condition of the economy, the  seasonal  factors  and  the  development  of  the  subsidized  employment.  The  decision  of  the employer  for  realization  of  labour  with  certain  quantitative  and  qualitative  characteristics  has  a decisive significance.

Those specific factors, together with the European and world trends for continuous growth of  the  requirements  for  employment  suitability,  determine  the  need  of  continuing  vocational training both for all-aged groups of labor force and considering also the territorial aspect.

The training for acquisition of vocational qualification by the people included in the labour market  is  organized  by  the  Employment  Agency.  The  total  number  of  the  carried  out    training courses in 2003 is 1977, which exceeds more than twice the number of the same in 2000.

The  main  part  of  graduated  some  vocational  training  in  2003  (29 294  persons)  were unemployed at the time they were included in. The number of employed people who graduated such training during the same period is 8 863 persons. In a comparison with 2000, the number of people that have graduated training for acquisition of vocational skills was totally 12 569 and the employed people were only 496³.

If we share the different kind of trainings, it prevails the acquisition of additional vocational skills - 67,4%, next comes the training for re-qualification - 22,9% and the training for obtaining initial vocational skills - 9,7%. There is a trend for increasing the number of training courses for additional vocational skills - in fact they were 2/3 of all carried out in 2003.

The Labour  Code -  LC  (1986,  last  amended  in  2004):  establishes  the  rights  and obligations  of  those  who  are  engaged  in  and  during  the  training  for  acquisition  and  raising  the qualification and re-qualification on the basis of agreement between the employer and employee;

The Public Education Act -PEA (1991): provides the main normative organization of the education; regulates the right of the citizens to continue raising their education and qualification; determines  the  role  and  place  of  the  State  Educational  Requirements  as  well  as  the  order  for issuance of documents for obtaining educational degree and skills;

The Vocational  Education  and  Training  Act -  VETA  (1999):  provides  the  normative organization  of  the  initial  and  continuing  vocational  training  with  a  main  goal  to  guarantee  the quality of the vocational education and training in conformity with the needs of the labour market and in compliance with the trends in the European Union; determines the functions of the system of vocational education and training (VET) to prepare people for realization in the economy and society, providing conditions for acquisition and permanent raising of the vocational skills;

The Higher  Education  Act -HEA  (1995):  regulates the organization, functions, administration and financing of the higher education in Republic of Bulgaria; provides conditions for  synchronization  of  the  Bulgarian  system  of  higher  education  with  the  European  trends  for access to a quality preparation, the possibilities for mobility within the system and the transparency of the acquired qualifications;

The Employment Promotion Act -  EPA  (2001):  formulates  the  transition  from  passive social assistance towards active measures of the labour market in order to increase the employment and raise the qualification of the labour force; introduces stimuli for employers to train employed for  updating  and  raising  their  qualification  and  to  train  unemployed,  in  the  context  of  life-long learning;  regulates  the  types  of  continuing  vocational  training  organized  by  the  Employment Agency and the conditions for its accomplishment;

The Crafts  Act - CA  (2001):  regulates  the  conditions  and  rules  for  training  crafts, including training by master at a concrete work place in the craft enterprise; determines the basic degrees of the craftsmen training;

The People's  Community  Centers Act -  PCCA  (1996):  regulates  the  activity  of  the community centers within the country for fulfillment of their main tasks, including expansion of the  people's  knowledge, united people with the values and achievements of the science, art and culture through training and creation of possibilities for studying;

The Convention on Technical and Vocational Education (signed in Paris on 10.11.1989, deposited by UNESCO, effective from 29.08.1991; in Bulgaria effective from 01.10.1994, the bill passed  by  Decision  of  the  Council  of  Ministers  №  236/15.06.1994)  -  our  country  confirms  its readiness to develop policies and strategies for technical and vocational education within the frame of the educational system that will provide conditions for acquisition of knowledge and skills by both youths and adults, which are needed for their economic and social development, as well as for the individual and cultural growth of the personality in the society.

### 3. Institutions Responsible for the Continuing Vocational Training

#### On the national level:

The Council of Ministers (CM) determines the policy of the State in the field of education and employment.

The Ministry  of  Education  and  Science (MES)  guides,  coordinates  and  controls  the accomplishment of the state policy in the field of vocational education and training. The Ministry regulates  and  methodically  guides  the  continuing  vocational  training  that  is  being  accomplished through  a  system  of  vocational  schools,  vocational  secondary  schools,  art  schools,  vocational colleges  and  the  higher  schools.  The  Minister  of  Education  and  Science  confirms  the  State Educational  Requirements  for  acquisition  of  qualifications  by  professions,  approves  the  List  of Professions for vocational education and training, provides conditions for implementation of the State  Educational  Requirements  to  the  system  of  vocational  education  and  training,  approves programmes for qualification in the Vocational Education and Training System.

The  National  Council  for  Promotion  of  Employment at  the  Minister  of  Labour  and Social  Policy  is  a  permanently  operating  body  for  cooperation  and  consultation  in  elaborating employment policy.

### 4. Institutions for Continuing Vocational Training

Institutions  for  formal  education  and  training  and  non-formal  training  carry  out  the continuing vocational training in the country.

Main institutions for continuing vocational training in the field of the formal education and training, according to the VETA, are the vocational schools, the vocational secondary schools, the vocational colleges and Centers for Vocational Training (CVT).

In  2003  the  network  of  vocational  institutions  in  the  country  included  470  schools, secondary  schools  and  colleges,  and  among  them  421  schools  are  of  the  State,  12  -  of  the Municipalities and 37 are private. In 179 vocational schools, secondary schools and colleges has been carried out continuing professional training.

The  National  Agency  for  Vocational  Education  and  Training  licenses  the  Centers  for Vocational Training and is monitoring their activities. All Centers licensed after year 2002 offer vocational training for acquisition of first, second and third level of vocational qualification At this stage 146 licensed Centers and 169 Branches are operating.

The  main  difficulties,  which  the  institutions  for  continuing  vocational  training  in  the formal  system  are  facing,  are  related  to  the  shortage  of  funds  necessary  to  update  the  material-technical  base;  lack  of  modern  educational  and  reference  literature;  insufficient  methodical background of the teachers and lecturers for work with adults; shortage of funds for raising the qualification  of  the  teachers,  especially  in  real  productive  conditions;  insufficiently  effective dialogue  with  the  employers  and  their  low  interest  in  participation  in  the  process  of  continuing vocational  training.  The  schools,  which  are  carrying  out  continuing  vocational  training,  have additional difficulties due to lack of mechanisms for decentralizing financial administration of the revenues from this activity.

The vocational schools, vocational secondary schools and colleges, as well as the licensed Centers for Vocational Training, certify the completed vocational training  by  documents complying  with  the  State  Educational  Requirement  for  documents  in  the  system  of  the  public education.

The  system  of  the  higher  education  carries  out  continuing  vocational  training  through specialized units at the higher schools. All higher schools in the country (a total of 51) have units for post-graduate qualification and units for training of persons aged above 16 in professions and specialties.

The enterprises carry out non-formal continuing vocational training of the own employees (28%).  The  training  is  in  the  form  of  courses  for  vocational  qualification,  on-the-job  training, participation in seminars, conferences, etc.

According to  the  People's  Health  Act,  the  employers  can  perform  vocational  training  of unemployed persons also, but under certain conditions.

Non-formal continuing vocational training offer also the Community Centers; such cases are  however  rare  despite  the  fact  that  they  have  potential  for  such  training.  Those  traditional Bulgarian cultural-educational associations can organize activities for extending the knowledge of the people and their adherence to the achievements of the science, art and culture. In the smaller towns  and  villages  they  are  the  only  institutions  providing  access  to  a  library,  Internet  and information. That's why, they have to be supported in order to contribute the development of the continuing vocational training.

As  a  whole,  the  challenges  before  the  system  of  the  continuing  vocational  training  are related  to:  improving  the  mechanisms  for  information  openness  and  feedback;  improving  the quality of training and approaching it to characteristics which secure access to the European labour market;  creating  mechanisms  for  motivation  both  the  users  of  qualification  services  and  the organizations  that  invest  in  the  continuing  vocational  training;  uniting  the  efforts  of  all  social partners,  the  bodies  of  the  local  government  and  the  non-governmental  organizations  that  have attitude to the development of human resources and the economic progress of the country.

## II. STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE

The goal of the development and improving of the continuing vocational training is:

Creation and perfection of conditions for acquisition, expansion and development of the vocational qualification of the labour force with a view to improve its suitability for employment, professional career and individual development .

In connection with the realization of the strategic goal and with a view of the prospects for development during the period 2005-2010, the following basic priorities are determined:

1. Improvement of the conditions for access to continuing vocational training.

2. Achievement of efficiency in the interaction between institutions involved in the continuing vocational training.

3. Assurance the high quality of the continuing vocational training.

4. Increasing the investments in the continuing vocational training.

5. Scientific assurance of the continuing vocational training.

## ІІІ.  ACTIVITIES FOR REALIZATION OF THE STRATEGIC OBJECTIVES

### PRIORITY № 1

#### Improvement of the conditions for access to the continuing vocational training

A necessary condition for inclusion of each person in the continuing vocational training is assuring of equal access opportunities, which do not depend on sex, age, place of residence, ethnic belonging, social and material status. For this reason, the forthcoming actions, aimed at improving the  access  to  the  continuing  vocational  training,  should  be  organized  in  a  system  providing  to people  free  access  to  various  levels,  forms  and  methods  for  acquisition  of  knowledge  and development  of  skills.  The  basic  principles  of  this  system  are:  openness,  diversity,  wide  scope, comparativeness,  measurability  of  the  results,  transparency,  and  mutual  acknowledgment  of  the qualifications.

For  development  of  the  conditions  for  access  to  the  continuing  vocational  training  it  is necessary to:

- o Monitoring and analyzing the trends related to participation of people aged above 16 years in continuing vocational training;

- o Creation of an informative system for demand and supply of continuing vocational training;

- o Improving the legal framework in respect of the access to continuing vocational training;

- o Activating the social environment for life-long learning;

- o Development and implementation of  a  programme  related  to  motivation  for  inclusion  in continuing vocational training, with a special stress on groups that are disadvantaged in the labour market;

- o Development  of  methods  and  conditions  for  open  training,  including  organization  of distance-training through the existing network as well as carrying out e-learning.

### PRIORITY № 2

#### Achievement  of  efficiency  in  the  interaction  and  coordination  of  the  institutions involved in the continuing vocational training

The  strategic significance of the continuing vocational training for improving  the competitiveness of the enterprises and the professional growth and adaptability of the labour force, presumes undertaking of mutual responsibilities by the institutions at national, regional and local levels  (Ministries;  organizations  of  employers,  workers  and  employees;  regional  and  local governments). The realization of this priority requires accomplishment of the following activities:

- o Creation of administrative capacity for coordinating the cooperation between the institutions and social partners on policies in the field of the continuing vocational training;

- o Elaboration of the legal framework for determination the responsibilities of the institutions involved in continuing vocational training;

- o Expanding the scope of the collective negotiation for the training with a view to improve the adaptability of the enterprises and their labour force to the labour market;

- o Creation  of  a  network  for  open  dialogue  between  institutions  involved  in  the  continuing vocational training on national, regional and local levels;

- o Expanding at regional and local levels the services in the field of the continuing vocational training, providing an accessible for the specific target groups' form.

### PRIORITY № 3

#### Assurance the high quality of the continuing vocational training .

The high quality is paramount necessity for the continuing vocational training in order to secure comparability with the European practices by 2010. The high quality of the training reflects directly  upon  the  suitability  for  employment  of  the  labour  force,  upon  the  productivity  and competitive  power  of  both  the  enterprises  and  labour  force  to  reduce  the  unemployment  and overcome  the  social  isolation. - o Improving  the  qualification  of  the  teaching  staff  in  the  field  of  continuing  vocational training;

- o Achieving high studying culture through informing, consulting and orientation;

### PRIORITY № 4

#### Increasing the investments in the continuing vocational training

The investments in the human capital in Bulgaria during the past years mark contradicting trends. Despite that a great part of the employers and individuals are aware about the importance of this  factor,  the  share  of  the  vocational  training  carried  out  is  still  insufficient.  It  is  necessary  to overcome the negative influence of the population's low income, the economic instability and the lack of understanding by some of the employers.

In this context essential is the following:

- o Achieving  of  awareness  by  the  staff  about  the  provided  by  the  employers  continuing vocational training; spreading good practices;

- o Introducing  new  measures  and  tax  stimuli  for  encouraging  the  employers  to  raise  the qualification of their workers and employees;

- o Introducing  effective  measures  for  encouraging  unemployed  to  participate  in  continuing vocational training, accounting for the specificity of regions with structural unemployment, rural regions and risk groups;

- o Raising  the  managerial  culture  of  the  employers  in  respect  of  elaboration  of  short-term, middle-term and long-term plans for development of the human resources in the small and medium-sized enterprises.

## ІV. FINANCIAL SECURITY OF THE CONTINUING VOCATIONAL TRAINING

Investing in the quality of the human resources is a component of the general social policy of Republic of Bulgaria⁵.

The analysis of the existing trends in the development of the continuing vocational training and  the  challenges  of  the  accession  to  the  European  Union  impose  the  conclusion  that  it  is necessary  to  invest  sensibly  more  in  the  training.  Accomplishment  of  the  objectives  and  the national priorities laid down in the Strategy requires significant financial resources.

As  a  whole,  an  increase  in  the  share  of  the  public  expenses  for  education  is  expected, including vocational training (for 2002 it is 4.6% of the GDP; according to preliminary data for 2003, the share is 4,9% of the GDP)⁶.

It is necessary to increase considerably the funds that the employers invest in updating and raising the vocational qualification of the employed in the enterprises labour force.

The  used  approach  for programme  orientation of  funds  from  the  Republican  Budget should  be  applied  on  a  common  basis.  This  will  allow  maximally  effective  realization  of  the financial means in achievement of the goals of the Strategy through the responsible institutions - МLSP, MES, MF, ME, MRDPW, MYS and other agencies and administrations. All of them will work for fulfillment of the basic priorities of the Strategy, in conformity with the time frame.

The  influx  of  funds  from international  aid will  continue  to  represent  a  significant contribution  to  securing  of  means  for  vocational  training  to  be  used  by  various  institutions  and target groups.

On this  ground,  for  the  period  2005-2010  is  envisaged  use  of  the  following  sources  for financing the continuing vocational training:

- o Funds  from  the  Republican  Budget,  allocated  to  responsible  institutions,  regional  and municipal administrations;

- o The PHARE programme - in the Section 'Social and Economic Cohesion';

- o Structural funds of the European Union (after 2007);

- o International resources;

- o Funds of employers, including application of schemes for cooperation between small and medium-sized enterprises, as well as uniting financial resources for carrying out training for professional qualification;

- o Own revenues of suppliers of professional training;

- o Personal funds for development of the individual labour force;

- o Other funds - of the Municipalities, local legal persons, non-governmental organizations, etc.

- o Sponsorships, donations etc.

The effective utilization of the above possibilities for financing the continuing vocational training presumes a high degree of coordination in this activity, in compliance with the priorities of the continuing vocational training on various levels.

## V. EXPECTED RESULTS

In the time of social-economic alterations in the society, it is necessary that the suppliers of continuing vocational training direct their activities toward the needs of the labour market. Closer cooperation between all institutions having attitude towards the continuing vocational training will raise  the  correspondence  of  the  training  courses  with  the  present  and  future  needs  of  the  labour market.

The implementation of the strategy should lead to:

- o Creation of active informative system for the demand and supply of continuing vocational training;

- o Improvement  of  the  information  and  motivation  of  people  for  participation  in the continuing vocational training;

- o Increase in the number of participants in the continuing vocational training;

- o Improvement  of  the  interaction  and  coordination  between  institutions  and  organizations responsible for the continuing vocational training on national, regional and local levels;

- o Improvement of the correspondence between demand and supply of vocational qualifications;

- o Strengthening the capacity of suppliers of professional training in respect of their marketing activity and the quality of the offered continuing vocational training;

- o Increasing the number of offered courses for continuing vocational training as a part of the labour market's active measures for achievement of sustained employment.

- o Improvement of the access to e-learning by building of infrastructures as a flexible training form and stimulus for development of the continuing vocational training;

- o Reduction of the regional discrepancies related to the employment and labour  market.

- o Growth of the  media  interest  and  activity  of  the  public  dialogue  on  the  problems  of  the continuing vocational training.

## GLOSSARY OF THE MAIN TERMS

Continuing vocational training - training of people older than 16 years to acquire, expand and increase their professional qualification with the aim to improve their employment suitability, support their professional career and individual development.

Formal  training -  Training  that  is  carried  out  within  the  frame  of  the  institutions  for vocational  training,  identified  in  art.  18  of  the  VETA,  and  is  characterized  by  obtaining  of  a nationally acknowledged document for certification of the acquired qualification.

Non-formal  training -  Training  that  is  carried  out  within  the  frames  of  each  organized activity outside the formal system.

Self-dependent studying - A process of studying (and self-education) during the entire life, by means of which are acquired knowledge and skills, adjustments, intellectual values of the every-day experience, through educational influence and sources of the surrounding social and material environment, including libraries and mass media.

Life-long learning - a process of learning and acquiring of knowledge by means of formal and non-formal training and studying, as well as through self-dependent studying, during the entire vital activity of the person.

The life-long learning is considered by the society to be one of the most important factors for  development  and  elaboration  of  the  professional  realization  and  for  encouraging  active participation of the people in the economic life. It is a necessary prerequisite for free movement of the labour force and a leading principle for further development of the system of education and training in Bulgaria.
