## VI. Questionnaire

### A. Laws and policies (preamble and paras 4 and 7 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### A1a. Please  describe any  specific national or sub-national laws, legislation or legal framework(s) in your country that govern the promotion of global citizenship education (GCED) and/or education for sustainable development (ESD). Explain whether GCED and/or ESD themes 1  are mentioned explicitly or are inferred and what types or levels of education are covered.

The Education Act (2010:800) states  that  the  education must  convey  and  anchor respect for human  rights  and  the  fundamental  democratic  values  that  Swedish  society  is  based  on. Furthermore, the education must be designed in accordance with fundamental democratic values and human rights, such as the inviolability of human life, the freedom and integrity of the individual, the equal value of all human beings, equality and solidarity between people.

In  the  curricula  and  syllabi  for  preschool,  pre-primary  and  primary,  secondary  education  and municipal adult education both GCED and ESD are covered.

Post-secondary and tertiary education as well as non-formal education are governed by specific laws and regulation or through regulation on government grants. GCED and/or ESD themes are usually included in those regulations. For example:

- In  the  Regulation (2015:218) of the government grant to liberal adult education, themes related to GCED are inferred.

- The higher vocational education act (covering tertiary, non-academic vocational education) promotes in general terms both GCED and ESD themes.

The Higher Education Act, which contains provisions relating to higher education institutions for which  the  accountable  authority  is  the  Government  (the  below  provision  is  valid  also  for independent higher education providers), stipulates the following:

- In  the  course  of  their  operations,  higher  education  institutions  shall  promote  sustainable development to assure for  present  and future  generations  a  sound  and  healthy  environment, economic and social welfare, and justice.

- Equality between women and men shall always be taken into account and promoted in the operations of higher education institutions.

- Furthermore, in their operations higher education institutions should promote understanding of other countries and of international circumstances.

- Higher education institutions shall also actively promote and widen recruitment to higher education.

The Qualifications Ordinance, annex 2 to the Higher Education Ordinance contains regulations on education outcomes for all higher education degrees. GCED and/or ESD related themes are mentioned or inferred in the outcomes for certain degrees. For example, for a Degree of Bachelor of Science in Nursing the student shall, i.a., demonstrate knowledge of social circumstances that affect the health of children, women and men, demonstrate knowledge of men's violence towards women and violence in close relationships. Another example is that for a Degree of Master of Arts/Science in Secondary/Upper Secondary Education the student shall, i.a., demonstrate the

#### A3a.  Please  describe  any  specific  current  national  or  sub-national  education  policies, frameworks  or  strategic  objectives  in  your  country  that  promote  global  citizenship education  (GCED)  and/or  education  for  sustainable  development  (ESD).  Explain whether GCED and/or ESD themes 2  are mentioned explicitly or are inferred and what types or levels of education are covered.

In general laws and regulations mentioned in section A1a also reflect education policies and strategic objectives.

The curricula of pre-primary, primary and secondary education, and municipal adult education, all cover Education for Sustainable Development and Global Citizenship Education, although those specific concepts are not mentioned explicitly. However, sub-concepts such as gender equality and human rights are mentioned explicitly.

As mentioned in section A1a post-secondary and tertiary education as well as non-formal education are governed by specific laws and regulation or through regulation on government grants. GCED and/or ESD themes are usually included in those regulations. National agencies or organisations responsible for the implementation may however define more specific objectives. For example, the specific concept of GCED is not mentioned explicitly in the Regulation (2015:218) of the government grant to liberal adult education. However, according to the regulation, the objective of the government grant to liberal adult education, is, amongst others, to:

-Support activities that contribute to strengthening and developing democracy

-Make it possible for an increased diversity of people to influence their life situation and create participative involvement in societal development.

-Bridge educational gaps and raise the level of education and cultural awareness in society.

In the Swedish National Council of Adult Education's terms for the government grant to liberal adult education (study associations and folk high schools), terms related to GCED, such as human rights and democratic values, are explicitly referred to.

According to the provisions of the Higher Education Act (1992:1434) universities and university colleges have to promote sustainable development. Sustainable development means that the present and future generations are ensured a healthy and good environment, economic and social welfare, and justice.

### B. Curriculum (paras 4 and 10-19 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### B1a. Please describe in which ways GCED and ESD are mainstreamed in school curricula. Indicate  whether  topics  are  taught  as  standalone  subjects  or  are  integrated  into  the curricula of other subjects. Explain whether GCED and/or ESD themes 3  are mentioned explicitly or are inferred and what types or levels of education are covered.

GCED and ESD are not standalone subjects but integrated into the curricula and syllabi. Neither GCED or ESD are mentioned  explicitly  but  are  inferred  in  the  different  curricula  and  syllabi. Sustainable development is mentioned though. The same applies for the preschool, compulsory school, preschool class and the school-age educare, for the compulsory school for pupils with learning disabilities,  for  the  Sami  school  and for the  compulsory special needs school. It also applies to the upper secondary school, the upper secondary school for students with learning disabilities, and to municipal adult education.

For the study associations and folk high schools of the mainly non-formal liberal adult education, there is no national or sub-national governance of the curricula and syllabi. Each study association or folk high school has the freedom the integrate GCED and ESD themes in the education and other activities offered, either as standalone subjects or integrated into other subjects.

Within higher vocational education there is no national or sub-national governance of learning outcomes in qualifications and course syllabi. These are decided by each education provider.

An overview of the work of HEI can be found here as the Swedish Higher Education Authority (UKÄ) was tasked by the Swedish government to conduct an evaluation of efforts by universities and university colleges to promote sustainable development, pursuant to the provisions of the Higher Education Act (1992:143) in March 2016: https://english.uka.se/about-us/publications/reports--guidelines/reports--guidelines/2018-02-15-how-swedish-heis-work-in-promoting-sustainable-development.html

### C. Teacher education (including the training of trainers and educators) (paras 4 and 33-37 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### C1a. Please describe in which ways GCED and ESD are mainstreamed in initial training and in  continuing  professional  development  of  teachers,  trainers  and  educators.  Indicate whether all teachers, trainers and educators or only some are required to receive training to teach GCED and/or ESD.

As described in A1, The Higher Education Act stipulates that:

- In  the  course  of  their  operations,  higher  education  institutions  shall  promote  sustainable development to assure for  present  and future  generations  a  sound  and  healthy  environment, economic and social welfare, and justice.

- Equality between women and men shall always be taken into account and promoted in the operations of higher education institutions.

- Furthermore, in their operations higher education institutions should promote understanding of other countries and of international circumstances.

- Higher education institutions shall also actively promote and widen recruitment to higher education.

In  addition,  GCED  and/or  ESD  related  themes  are  mentioned  or  inferred  in  the  qualification outcomes for degrees in education:

- Students shall demonstrate the capacity to communicate and instil core educational values, including  human  rights  and  the  fundamental  democratic  values,  demonstrate  the  capacity  to prevent and restrain discrimination and other forms of harassment of children/pupils, demonstrate the capacity to respect, communicate and instil a gender equal and equal rights perspective in educational  processes,  From  1  Jan,  2021,  students  shall  also  demonstrate  the  capacity  to communicate and reflect  on  issues  relating  to  identity,  sexuality  and  relationships.  (the  latter provision is valid for all teacher education programmes except pre-school).

- Students  shall  also  demonstrate  the  capacity  to  make  assessments  in  educational processes on the basis of relevant scientific, social and ethical aspects with particular respect for human rights, especially children´s rights according to the Convention on the Rights of the Child, and sustainable development.

As  for  continuing  professional  development,  the  Education  Act  specifies  that  the  provider  of education must ensure that the staff at preschool units and school units are given opportunities for  competence  development.  Moreover,  the  provider  must  ensure  that  preschool  teachers, teachers and other staff at the preschool and school units have the necessary insights into the regulations that apply to the school system.

The  Swedish  National  Agency  for  Education  provides  web-based  modules  for  continuing professional  development,  some  of  which  includes  issues  concerning  GCED  and  ESD.  For instance, there is one module about promoting equal treatment.

For the liberal adult education, there is no national regulation regulating the specific competence or diploma of teachers at folk high schools or activity leaders at study associations. However, insight into the regulations that apply to the liberal adult education as well as insight into questions

### D. Student assessment (paras 4 and 5 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### D1a.  Please describe in which ways GCED and ESD are covered in student assessments and examinations. Indicate whether students are assessed only on their knowledge of and skills  related  to  the  topics  being  tested  or  also  on  their  values,  attitudes  and/or behaviours.

Regarding pre-primary, primary and secondary education: Values and attitudes are almost exclusively described in general sections of the curriculum (core curricula) with rules and guidelines concerning the entire education, whole school staff and all subjects. Syllabus covers mainly the fields of skills and knowledge/critical understanding. Further so are pupil values and attitudes not assessed on an individual level like knowledge and skills. Values and attitudes are monitored on a more structural level by the Swedish School Inspectorate. Knowledge and skills are assessed on an individual level by grades and ongoing formative assessment.

If GCED or ESD is part of the core content and/or the knowledge requirements teachers have to assess the child/pupil accordingly.  It is therefore difficult to fill in D2 since those themes are not generally included in all subjects as a knowledge requirement even though preschools and schools always work with the themes.

It is a responsibility of each higher education institution to design examinations and student assessments in order to ascertain that students meet the learning outcomes enumerated in the Higher Education Ordinance (see answer to A1a). As well, HEI:s in their operations are to promote the aspects enumerated in the Higher Education Act (see answer to A1A).

### F. Technical and vocational education and training (paras 25-29 of the 1974 Recommendation )

#### F1 Please  describe  any  major  initiatives  that  have  been  undertaken  to  promote  GCED and/or  ESD  in  technical  and  vocational  education  and  training.  Describe  briefly  the outcomes and impact of such initiatives and provide examples of good practices.

In Sweden, vocational education and training is included in upper secondary school and adult education at upper secondary level and use the same curricula. All answers concerning upper secondary school above also apply to vocational education and training at upper secondary level.

In 2017, the Swedish government tasked the National Agency for Education with proposing changes to the curricula for upper secondary school, upper secondary special school and adult education to clarify the school's mission to counteract traditional gender patterns, to shape and convey values such as equality between women and men and that actively and consciously promote their equal rights and opportunities. In 2018, The National Agency presented their proposals, one of which clarifies that the school's gender equality mission also includes workplace-based learning.

In 2018 the government tasked the National Agency for Education with analysing, and if necessary, suggest how, teaching in the knowledge area of sex education in the compulsory school system, upper secondary school, upper secondary special school and adult education better can be supported by clarifications in the curricula for the school forms. In 2019 the National Agency presented proposals that emphasise that the area should be treated regularly and that the education shall promote students' health and well-being, strengthen the ability to make conscious and independent choices, contribute to the understanding of one's own and others' rights, convey the importance of voluntariness and reciprocity, highlighting power relations linked to gender and notions of honour and develop students' critical attitudes to representations of relationships and sexuality. The proposals include all vocational education and training at upper secondary level.

Furthermore, in Sweden the folk high schools of the liberal adult education provide vocational education and training. All answers above concerning liberal adult education therefore also apply to vocational education and training.

### I. Any other comments

#### I1 Please indicate whether your country has faced any constraints, in particular related to global or national  crises,  including  natural  disasters,  in  implementing  the  1974 Recommendation during the reporting period.

#### I2 Please provide any other relevant information or clarifications regarding the implementation of the 1974 Recommendation or the mainstreaming of GCED and ESD in your country's education system.

In Sweden, public-sector HEIs have considerable autonomy within a system of management by objectives. Within the framework of national regulations that apply to the higher education sector, primarily the Higher Education Act and the Higher Education Ordinance, HEIs take most decisions themselves. These decisions cover such areas as organisation; internal allocation of resources; educational offerings; educational content and design; how many students are admitted and what research is conducted. Qualifications are regulated in the Qualifications Ordinance, stipulating for each higher education degree the knowledge and skills that the student should have attained in order to be awarded the specific degree. It is up to the HEI to determine how to ascertain the student's knowledge and skills, for example, they are free to design the curriculum and decide on examination and student assessment methods.
