Please provide a brief description of the provisions:

The Fundamental Law of Hungary recognizes national minorities in Hungary as constituent parts of the state, and the Fundamental Law - among others - undertakes the fostering and guarding of the languages and cultures of national minorities in Hungary. It recognizes their right to freely express and preserve their identity, use their mother tongue, use names in their own languages individually and collectively, nurture their own cultures and receive education in their own mother tongues.

In  line  with  the  Fundamental  Law,  the  detailed  rules  applicable  to  the  teaching  and  education  of national minorities in Hungary are set out by the act on the rights of national minorities and the Act on  National  Public  Education.  In  accordance  with  this,  participation  in  minority  teaching  and education is voluntary in Hungary, and is provided on the basis of the parents' demands belonging to the minority in question. On the initiative of at least eight parents of the same nationality made in writing,  the  local  government  having  this  duty  will  be  responsible  for  organising  the  teaching  and education of national minorities. The parents have the right to choose the form of national minority kindergarten education (kindergarten with the mother tongue and the national minority language, bilingual national minority kindergarten education and care, and Roma national minority kindergarten  education  conducted  solely  in  Hungarian),  and  the  form  of  national  minority  school teaching-education (teaching-education in the national minority language, bilingual minority national teaching-education, language teaching and education for minorities, Roma/Gipsy minority teachingeducation in Hungarian, supplementary minority teaching-education). It is the duty of the state to ensure  the  legal,  personal  and  objective  conditions  of  teaching-education  with  regard  to  parental demands.

Effective legal provisions give a right of consent to national minority self-governments in the owners' decision-making process concerning the most important questions relating to minority teaching and education. We also consider it important that national minority self-governments may take over and maintain public educational institutions. A local national minority self-government may delegate a member  to  the  school  board,  and  the  Association  of  National  Minority  Self-Governments  may delegate  a  member  to  the  newly  formed  educational  district  councils.  Additionally,  the  National Minority  Council  operating  as  a  consultative  body  of  the  Minister  responsible  for  education  may express their opinion on minority teaching-education related measures and drafts. We also think it is important that legal drafts are given to national minority self-governments for commenting during the  administrative  consultation.  Based  on  the  above  it  can  be  ascertained  that  legal  provisions continue to give the right to national self-governments concerned in order to express their opinion on national minority teaching-education.

National  minority  kindergarten  education  shall  be  organised  with  consideration  to  the  basic principles set out in the national minority directive and the national basic program of Kindergarten Education. The content of school teaching-education is defined by the national minority directive and the  national  minority  framework  curricula. In the case of most national minorities, national minority teacher training is ensured at the Hungarian institutions of higher education. They also have the right to be employed with their teachers' degrees obtained in their mother countries or countries of that language as the official language after the naturalisation of their diploma. The Act on National Public Education allows for several exemptions in favour of professionally trained teachers. If there is no proper national higher education for teachers of  some specific subjects, or if a task cannot be performed due to the lack of properly graduated teachers,  kindergartens,  schools  or  boarding  facilities  may  employ  persons  with  (non-minority) degrees  as  national  minority  teachers  if  they  have  at  least  an  intermediate  level  complex  staterecognized language certificate or an equivalent document in the given national minority language. The Act also allows for teachers with foreign degrees to be employed for a maximum of three years if they are allowed to work as teachers in the country which has issued their diploma, and if they made their studies in the language of the given national minority.

In  line  with  the  applicable  legal  provisions,  national  minority  self-governments  may  establish  and operate teaching-educational institutions, and may take over the management of national minority teaching-educational institutions established by other maintainers.

As a result, national-level minority self-governments have taken over 27, while local-level minority self-governments have taken over 63 public educational institutions over the past years. The central budget provides priority subsidy for the maintenance of such institutions.

In  national  minority school teaching-education, the acquisition of the knowledge falling within the scope of ethnology in addition to the language and literature of the given national minority shall be obligatory. In the case of the Roma, the teaching of the Romani or the Beash language shall only be ensured  if  requested  by  the  parents.  (The  main  reason  for  this  is  that  a  major  part  of  the  Roma community in  Hungary  speaks  Hungarian,  and  does  not  speak  any  languages  used  by  the  Roma). Ethnology shall be taught in the language of the national minority. It is only possible in the case of the Roma to learn ethnology in Hungarian for the reasons described above.

The  National  Minority  Teachers'  Programme  elaborated  by  the  Parliamentary  Committee  for National Minorities in Hungary facilitates the provision of national minority teachers. The programme aims at increasing the number of young people opting for a national minority teacher carrier, and the level of national minority teacher training, as well as making language teaching more efficient, and keeping national minority teachers in this career by recognizing them at a higher level. The program also includes scholarships for young people in national minority teachers' programmes, support of national minority teacher training facilities through applications, and a significant rise in the level of national  minority  allowances. #### Please briefly describe the existing legal and policy framework that protects the right to quality education for refugees and migrants in your country.

Section 92 of the Act CXC of 2011 on National Public Education rules the educational conditions of the  non-Hungarian  minor  citizens  in  Hungary  until  their  completion  of  compulsory  education  as follows:

Non-Hungarian minor citizens shall be entitled to access pre-school educational services and shall be subject to compulsory education in Hungary if they

a)  have  one  of  the  following  status:  refugee,  beneficiary  of  subsidiary  protection,  beneficiary  of temporary  protection,  received  by  specific  provision  of  the  Act  on  Asylum  as  of  the  date  of submission of their request for recognition,

b) are entitled to enjoy the right to freedom of movement and stay in accordance with the Act on the Entry and Stay of Persons Enjoying the Right to Freedom of Movement and Stay,

c) fall under the scope of the Act on the Entry and Stay of Nationals of Third Countries and have an immigration  status  or  have  been  granted  settlement,  or  are  authorised  to  stay  in  the  territory  of Hungary. Rights regulated in points b) and c) can be exercised if the parent and the child have been issued a stay permit exceeding 3 months.

Most of foreigners living in Hungary are Hungarians having come from neighbouring countries. Due to the same ethnicity, the common language, culture and history, the migrant community in Hungary dominated  by  Hungarians  from  transborder  territories  did  not  cause  such  social  problems  as immigrant  populations  with  different  cultures,  languages  or  religions  did  in  Western  Europe.  The mass  migration  experienced  in  2015  did  not  cause  a  serious  trouble  in  the  Hungarian  public educational system, because migrants arriving here did not consider Hungary as a target country but rather as a transit route and moved further after a few days or weeks - except for a tiny proportion.

Most of those economic immigrants whose first language is not Hungarian - and stay in Hungary for a longer period of time - have their children enrolled in foreign or international schools.

There  are  no  prescribed  or  uniform  methods  as  for  the  assessment  and  placement  of  migrant children.  Schools  integrate  plans  and  solutions  for  promoting  the  integration  of  children  with different cultural backgrounds into their pedagogical programmes and local curricula, which take into consideration the local conditions and needs. Schooling of migrant children takes place in the district school (with obligatory acceptance) according to their place of residence.

Education for children seeking asylum takes place in the premises of the transit zones, as during the official  procedure  they  have  to  stay  within  the  zones.  In  the  Tompa  and  Röszke  transit  zones  the academic  year  is  planned  according  to  the  ministerial  decree  regulating  it  in  general.  Education started in September 2017 continuing in the current academic year.
